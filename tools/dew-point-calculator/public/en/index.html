Dew Point Calculator

Calculate the dew point, absolute humidity and comfort band from air temperature and relative humidity with the Magnus formula. The dew point measures the actual water content of the air: the same 70% relative humidity holds very different amounts of water at 15 °C and at 30 °C, and a surface colder than the dew point collects condensation.

Comfort band table and formula notes

The comfort band is looked up from the rounded dew point, lower bound inclusive, upper bound exclusive:

Dew point (°C)Band
Below 10Dry
10 to below 13Comfortable
13 to below 16Mostly comfortable
16 to below 18Slightly humid
18 to below 21Humid
21 to below 24Oppressive
24 and aboveExtremely oppressive

The formula is the Magnus form over water with pinned constants a = 17.27, b = 237.7 °C and es(0 °C) = 6.112 hPa: first γ = ln(RH/100) + aT/(b+T), then the dew point Td = bγ/(a − γ); the saturation vapor pressure is es = 6.112 × exp(aT/(b+T)), the actual vapor pressure e = es × RH/100, and the absolute humidity is 216.7 × e/(273.15 + T) g/m³. Each output is rounded once, at the end, to one decimal place.

The over-water form is used throughout: below 0 °C the result is the dew point over supercooled water, not the frost point. This is an approximate model with no guaranteed error bound across the input range; use low-temperature and very-low-humidity results as rough guidance. The downloaded report is always named dew-point-report.json; its content is the result as compact JSON with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code units and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

Does the dew point equal the air temperature at 100% RH?
Yes — with the pinned formula the two are exactly equal at 100% RH.
Is the below-zero result a frost point?
No — the over-water Magnus form is used throughout, so below 0 °C the result is the dew point over supercooled water, not the frost point.
How does dew point differ from relative humidity?
The dew point measures the actual water content of the air; relative humidity also depends on temperature, so the same 70% can mean very different amounts of water.
