How it is calculated, and formats

There are nine standard sizes: 3×5, 4×6, 5×7, 6×8, 8×10 and 10×12 photos, plus A5, A4 and A3. Matching is orientation-free: the image's long side goes to the paper's long side, the short side to the short side. The largest printable size is the pixel count divided by the target DPI, in inches to 2 decimals and in centimetres to 1 decimal; the pixels a size requires are the size in inches times the target DPI, rounded up; each side's DPI is the pixels divided by that side in inches, and the effective DPI is the smaller side, rounded to 1 decimal. Quality bands follow the rounded effective DPI: 300 and above Excellent, 200 and above Good, 150 and above Acceptable, anything below Poor.

The downloaded report is always named photo-print-size-report.json. Its content is the result as compact JSON, with object keys sorted by UTF-16 code units recursively and one trailing line feed. This tool assumes full-frame printing without cropping and computes per-side DPI for the whole frame. A print shop may crop or add borders instead; check with the shop.

Frequently asked questions

Why do landscape and portrait photos get the same verdicts?
The size table is orientation-free — long side to long side, short side to short side — so orientation never changes printability.
Are the quality bands an industry standard?
No. They are common rules of thumb for a typical viewing distance (about arm's length); large prints are usually viewed from farther away, so below 150 DPI can still look fine.
What if the photo's aspect ratio differs from the paper's?
This tool assumes full-frame printing without cropping and computes per-side DPI for the whole frame. A print shop may crop or add borders instead; check with the shop.
Do I need to upload my photo?
No, and there is no upload feature. You only type the pixel width and height from the file's properties; everything is computed offline on this page.
What about viewing on a screen?
Screen sharpness is the Screen PPI Calculator's job; this tool only covers printing on paper.
