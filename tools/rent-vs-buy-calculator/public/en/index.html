How the estimate works and the fixed rules

The mortgage uses equal monthly instalments with an internal monthly split (the monthly table is not shown): the payment is rounded to whole dollars and the last month pays the balance off exactly. Rent compounds yearly, the home price rises or falls yearly, and the down payment's opportunity cost compounds yearly; the opportunity cost counts on the buying side only. The horizon in years is both the mortgage term and the comparison horizon. The breakeven year is the first year the difference reaches zero or above; whether it falls back afterwards, check the difference column of the yearly table.

All calculation happens locally in this page; nothing is uploaded, stored, or recorded. The result only reflects the assumptions you enter.

Frequently asked questions

What is opportunity cost?
Buying locks the down payment into the house, while renting assumes that money grows at the rate you enter; the forgone growth counts as part of buying's cost.
Once past the breakeven year, is buying always cheaper?
Not necessarily. The breakeven year is the first year buying catches up with renting; check the difference column of the yearly table for later years.
Is this buying advice?
No. It only estimates from the assumptions you enter, excludes taxes, agent fees, renovation, maintenance, management fees and loan-to-value limits, and is not a bank quotation.
