Calculation rules and limits
  • Each year's withdrawal is taken at the start of the year; the remaining balance then earns that year's return. Withdrawals grow with inflation from year 2.
  • Amounts are computed in exact integer cents, rounded to cents each year; the simulation caps at 100 years.
  • 4% rule mode: target portfolio = annual spending × 25. It runs no yearly table and takes no return or inflation assumption.
  • Money fields: decimal numbers with at most 2 decimal places and at most 16 characters each; no spaces, thousands commas, currency symbols, exponents or expressions.
  • Annual return is between -50 and 100; annual inflation is between 0 and 100. Deflation is not accepted.
  • Data stays only in the current page draft; leaving the page or switching language discards it. Nothing is stored or uploaded.

FAQ

Is the withdrawal taken at the start or the end of the year?

Each year's withdrawal is taken at the start of the year and the remaining balance then earns that year's return; a final partial year withdraws only the remaining balance, with no return.

Does the 4% rule guarantee the money lasts?

No. It is a rule of thumb from 30 years of US historical data in Bengen (1994) and the Trinity study (1998); future markets, taxes and lifespans can differ.

Why stop at 100 years?

100 years is the simulation cap to avoid an endless table; if a balance remains after 100 fully funded years it is reported as not depleted within the horizon.
