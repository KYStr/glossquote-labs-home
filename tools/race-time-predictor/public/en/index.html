Race Time Predictor

Enter a known race result and use the Riegel formula to predict finish times and per-kilometre pace for 5K, 10K, the half marathon and the marathon. A prediction is an estimate, not a promised finish time.

How it is calculated, and the input format

The calculation always uses the Riegel formula: the predicted time is T2 = T1 × (D2/D1)^1.06, where T1 and D1 are the known result's time and distance, D2 is the target distance, and the exponent 1.06 is a fixed constant in this tool. Predicted seconds are rounded half-up to a whole second once, from the unrounded raw value; each per-kilometre pace is likewise computed from the unrounded raw seconds divided by the distance in kilometres and rounded half-up to 0.1 s — never derived back from the predicted seconds. The known distance itself is one of the prediction rows, and its predicted value is exactly the input.

Distances are always entered and shown in metres, and pace is always per kilometre. Times at or above one hour display as H:MM:SS, otherwise M:SS; pace is rounded once more to a whole second and shown as M:SS. Inputs accept ASCII digits, one decimal point and time colons only: commas, spaces, signs, exponents and full-width digits are format errors, and the tool never guesses. The downloaded report is always named race-time-predictor-report.json; its content is the result as compact JSON with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code units and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Riegel formula?
The predicted time is T2 = T1 × (D2/D1)^1.06; the exponent 1.06 is a fixed constant in this tool.
How accurate is the prediction?
It is an estimate that assumes equivalent training and fitness across distances; it gets less reliable as the distance ratio grows.
Can I predict distances beyond the marathon?
Custom distances are capped at 42,195 metres in this tool; ultramarathon predictions are not offered.

Everything is calculated locally in this page; your input is never uploaded, stored, or logged.
