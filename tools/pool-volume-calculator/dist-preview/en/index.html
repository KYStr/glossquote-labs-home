Volume result

Estimated for an ideal shape with vertical walls. Wall thickness, steps, and the actual water level (usually below the rim) are not included; treat the result as an estimate.

Litres and cubic metres are each rounded once from the same unrounded volume; the litre figure is the more precise one.

How it is calculated, and formats

Average depth: a single depth is used as-is; a sloped floor averages the shallow- and deep-end depths (add first, then divide by 2). For a uniformly sloped rectangular floor the average is exact. Volume: rectangular is (length × width) × average depth; circular uses radius r = diameter ÷ 2, ((π × r) × r) × average depth; oval uses semi-axes a = length ÷ 2 and b = width ÷ 2, ((π × a) × b) × average depth. π is the double-precision Math.PI.

Cubic metres are rounded to 2 decimals, litres and US gallons to whole numbers, and total fill minutes to a whole number — each rounded once (half-up) from the same unrounded volume; 1 m³ = 1000 litres and 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 litres. Fill time is split once from the single rounded total minutes into hours and minutes, so minutes are always 0–59. The downloaded report is always named pool-volume-report.json; its contents are the result as compact JSON with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code units and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

How is a sloped floor handled?
The shallow- and deep-end depths are averaged; for a uniformly sloped rectangular floor this is exact, and only an estimate for uneven floors.
Why don't litres equal the displayed cubic metres × 1000?
Both are rounded once from the same unrounded volume; for a tiny pool the display can read 0 m³ and 1 litre, and the litre figure is the more precise one.
Can it work out chemical dosing, such as chlorine?
No. This tool calculates water volume only; chemical dosing is out of scope.
Can it handle kidney-shaped or irregular pools?
No. Only ideal rectangular, circular, and oval shapes are supported.
