The AHAM rule and the fixed calculation

Required smoke CADR (CFM) = room volume (ft³) × target air changes per hour ÷ 60, rounded up to whole CFM (a minimum must never round down); AHAM suggested room size (sq ft) = smoke CADR × 1.55. The 2/3 rule and the 1.55 factor are two directions of one relationship: required CADR = area × 2/3, suggested room = CADR × 1.55; the first is 5.0 ACH, the second 4.8 ACH, about 3% apart, and this tool defaults to the stricter 5.0, with 4.8 available as a manual choice.

When the ceiling is higher than 8 ft, required CADR scales with ceiling height (ceiling feet ÷ 8). Only smoke CADR is used for sizing (the smallest particle size, the strictest); if a spec sheet lists only m³/h, divide by 1.6990 to get CFM first. Marketing coverage is often quoted at 1–2 air changes per hour, while AHAM uses 4.8–5.0, so the two cannot be compared directly.

Frequently asked questions

Why smoke CADR, not dust or pollen CADR?
Smoke is the smallest of the three particle sizes and the strictest; the AHAM room-size rule is written against smoke CADR.
Are 1.55 and 2/3 the same rule?
They are two directions of one relationship: required CADR = area × 2/3, suggested room = CADR × 1.55; the first is 5.0 ACH, the second 4.8 ACH, about 3% apart, and this tool defaults to the stricter 5.0.
The box claims a bigger coverage than AHAM suggests — can I trust it?
Marketing coverage is often quoted at 1–2 air changes per hour, while AHAM uses 4.8–5.0; compare smoke CADR, not coverage claims.
What if my ceiling is higher than 8 ft?
Required CADR scales with ceiling height (ceiling feet ÷ 8); a taller room needs a bigger purifier.
