How conversion works

A scale of 1:N means 1 unit on the map equals N units in reality. Forward: real distance (cm) = map cm × N, then converted to metres (÷100) and kilometres (÷1000). Reverse: real distance is first converted to centimetres (metres ×100, kilometres ×100,000), then map cm = real cm ÷ N.

Rounding happens only at output: metres to 2 decimal places, kilometres to 3 decimal places, reverse-computed map centimetres to 2 decimal places, all half-up. The primary unit is decided on the exact unrounded value: below exactly 1000 m the primary unit is metres, at 1000 m or above it is kilometres; reverse results use centimetres. Frequently asked questions

What does a 1:25000 scale mean?
1 cm on the map equals 250 m (25,000 cm) in reality.
Why show both metres and kilometres?
Metres read better for short distances and kilometres for long ones; the primary unit switches at exactly 1000 m on the unrounded value.
Can I enter a non-integer map distance?
Yes, up to 3 decimal places, for example 2.5 cm.
