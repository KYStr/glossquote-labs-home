How it is calculated, and the input format

The order is fixed: tax is the bill amount times the tax rate; the tip is the chosen base (the pre-tax amount, or the bill plus the already-rounded tax) times the tip percent; the total is the three added together. Money is computed in integer cents and percentages in integer milli-percent (percent × 1000), with no floating point anywhere; tax and tip each round half-up exactly once, and the total and the split never round again.

How the split works: the total is divided by the number of people; the quotient is everyone's base share, and each leftover cent goes to the next person in order, so shares differ by at most one cent and always add up to the exact total. Switching the tip base changes only the tip and the total — never the tax.

Inputs accept ASCII digits and one decimal point only: commas, spaces, signs, exponents and full-width digits are format errors, and the tool never guesses. Amounts are always shown as an integer part with exactly two decimals, with no thousands separators and no currency symbol. The downloaded report is always named bill-split-report.json; its content is the result as compact JSON with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code units and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

Pre-tax or post-tax tip?
Customs differ — in the US the pre-tax amount is customary; this tool makes the base a choice, and the tax itself is always calculated on the bill amount.
Why do the first payers pay one cent more?
The total rarely divides evenly; each leftover cent goes to the next person in order, so the shares always add up to the exact total and differ by at most one cent.
What currency is this in?
The tool is currency-neutral: inputs and results use the same money unit, always with two decimal places.

Everything is calculated locally in this page; your input is never uploaded, stored, or logged.
