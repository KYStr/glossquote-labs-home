How categories are decided

The table follows AHA/ACC 2017: Normal is systolic < 120 and diastolic < 80; Elevated is systolic 120–129 and diastolic < 80; Stage 1 Hypertension is systolic 130–139 or diastolic 80–89; Stage 2 Hypertension is systolic ≥ 140 or diastolic ≥ 90; Hypertensive Crisis is systolic > 180 and/or diastolic > 120 (strictly greater — 180/120 itself is Stage 2). When systolic and diastolic fall in different categories, the higher category applies; the check order (crisis → Stage 2 → Stage 1 → Elevated → Normal, first match wins) implements that rule.

Frequently asked questions

Can one reading diagnose hypertension?
No; this classifies a single reading. Diagnosis needs repeated measurements and a physician's evaluation.
If systolic and diastolic fall in different categories, which one applies?
The higher category.
Is 180/120 a hypertensive crisis?
No; a crisis is systolic above 180 or diastolic above 120 (strictly greater), so 180/120 itself is Stage 2.
