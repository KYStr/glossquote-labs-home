How it is calculated, and formats

Deposits are always made at the end of the month (ordinary annuity): a deposit made this month starts earning interest next month. Mode A solves the monthly deposit from the term with the closed-form annuity formula; mode B solves the months to reach the goal with a logarithmic estimate corrected by exact rational comparison. All arithmetic uses integer cents and integer basis points exactly — no floating point decides any digit. Monthly amounts and the final month's smaller deposit are always rounded up to the cent; balances are shown half-up. The solving horizon is 1,200 months (100 years); beyond it the tool reports out of range instead of an astronomical month count. The downloaded report is always named savings-goal-report.json and holds the input and result as compact JSON, object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code units, with one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

When are deposits made?
At the end of each month (ordinary annuity); a deposit starts earning interest the following month.
Why is the monthly amount rounded up to the cent?
So the plan never falls a cent short; depositing the displayed amount ends at or slightly above the goal. The final month's smaller deposit follows the same rule, so the displayed interest can be under a cent below the exact figure.
Is the return guaranteed?
No. The rate is your own assumption and the result is only a projection under it, not a promise of any actual return.
How is this different from the Compound Interest Calculator?
That tool simulates growth month by month with a full table, and its answers come from that cent-rounded simulation; this tool solves only the two inverse questions with unrounded closed-form math, so the two can differ by a few cents on the same inputs.
