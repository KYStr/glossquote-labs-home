Overtime pay breakdown

How it is calculated, and the legal basis

The hourly rate is monthly salary ÷ 30 days ÷ 8 hours; one day's wage is monthly salary ÷ 30. Weekday overtime follows Labor Standards Act Article 24(1): 4/3× for the first 2 hours and 5/3× for the next 2. Rest-day work follows Article 24(2): since 1 March 2018 it is counted by actual hours worked — 4/3× for the first 2 hours, 5/3× for hours 3–8, and 8/3× for hours 9–12. National-holiday work follows Articles 37 and 39: any attendance up to 8 hours earns one extra day's pay, and hours beyond 8 use the weekday overtime rates.

Amounts are computed as exact fractions; each category subtotal is rounded half-up to whole NT$ and the total is the sum of the three subtotals. Hours counted toward the monthly overtime cap are weekday overtime + actual rest-day hours + holiday hours beyond 8; above 46 hours a note is shown (with union or labor-management agreement the cap may extend to 54 hours in a single month). Weekly-rest-day work, compensatory-time conversion, and flexible working-hour schemes are out of scope.

Every field accepts ASCII decimal strings only: the salary is an integer, and hours are integers or one-decimal values whose decimal digit is .0 or .5. The downloaded report is always named overtime-pay-report.json and contains the result as compact JSON, with keys sorted by UTF-16 code unit order and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

How are rest-day hours counted?
Since 1 March 2018 the deemed-hours rule was abolished; rest-day pay and hours use actual hours worked.
How is the hourly rate derived?
For monthly-paid workers, monthly salary ÷ 30 days ÷ 8 hours.
What if holiday work is under 8 hours?
Any holiday work up to 8 hours earns one extra day's pay.
