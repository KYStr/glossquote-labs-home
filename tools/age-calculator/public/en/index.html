How it is calculated

All arithmetic is integer calendar arithmetic (Julian day numbers) with no device clock or timezone involved: the same input always gives the same output. The total days are the difference of the two day numbers.

Years, months and days use a fixed borrow convention: when the days fall short they borrow the full length of the month before the reference month; when the months fall short they borrow twelve months from the years. A February 29 birth observes March 1 as its birthday in non-leap years; the next birthday is the first observed birthday on or after the reference date.

Frequently asked questions

Born on February 29 — when is the birthday in a non-leap year?
This tool observes it on March 1 in non-leap years, and on February 29 in leap years.
How are the days counted?
When the days fall short, they borrow the full length of the month before the reference month — the fixed convention of this tool; other tools may differ.
Can the reference date be in the future?
Yes — for example, to find the age on a school-entry date or an anniversary; any valid date after the birth date works.
