Breakeven Calculator

Enter monthly fixed costs, price per unit and variable cost per unit to find how many units you must sell, and how much revenue you need, to break even; add an expected volume to see the margin of safety and expected profit. All amounts use one currency of your choice; the tool does not convert or label a currency.

Costs and price

Monthly fixed costs, such as rent, subscriptions and wages. 0–999,999,999.99, up to two decimal places.

The selling price of one unit. 0.01–999,999,999.99, up to two decimal places; it must be above the variable cost per unit.

The variable cost of one unit, such as materials, packaging and delivery. 0–999,999,999.99, up to two decimal places; it must be below the price per unit.

Optional. How many units you expect to sell, a whole number from 0 to 100,000,000; leave blank to skip the margin of safety.

Margin of safety

Amounts are computed exactly in integer cents, without floating point; the breakeven volume is always rounded up — one unit fewer still loses money. The downloaded report is always named breakeven-report.json and holds the result as compact JSON, with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code unit and one trailing line feed.

How it is calculated, and the formats

Contribution margin per unit = price per unit − variable cost per unit; contribution margin ratio = contribution margin per unit ÷ price per unit; breakeven volume (exact) = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit; the whole-unit figure is the exact figure rounded up, or 0 when fixed costs are 0; breakeven revenue (exact) = fixed costs × price per unit ÷ contribution margin per unit, rounded to the cent; whole-unit revenue = whole-unit volume × price per unit. Margin of safety = expected volume − breakeven volume (whole units), with the percentage over the expected volume; expected profit = expected volume × contribution margin per unit − fixed costs. Displayed rounding always uses half-up, applied exactly once to the unrounded rational value.

Amount format: digits, at most one decimal point, 1–2 decimal places; no sign, space, thousands separator or exponent; no leading zeros in the integer part except a lone 0. Expected volume is a whole number. When the price is at or below the variable cost there is no breakeven point, and the tool says so instead of showing a number.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the breakeven volume always rounded up?
Selling one unit fewer leaves part of the fixed costs uncovered, so it still loses money; the whole-unit figure is the first volume that does not lose money.
Why are there two revenue figures?
The exact figure assumes fractional units could be sold; the whole-unit figure is the actual revenue at the first volume that breaks even, and they differ by less than one unit's price.
What if the price is at or below variable cost?
Each unit contributes zero or less, so no volume can cover fixed costs and no breakeven point exists; the tool explains this instead of showing a number.
What is the margin of safety?
How far the expected volume sits above (or below) the breakeven volume, in units and percent, together with the profit or loss at that volume.
