Conversion result

Table volumes use straight-sided geometry at the nominal dimensions; real pans taper and have rounded corners, so actual capacity differs slightly. Nominal rows (Bundt / tube) use the labelled capacity from a standard conversion chart, not a geometric value.

How it is calculated, and the formats used

Factor = target pan volume ÷ source pan volume. Table volumes are straight-sided geometry at the nominal dimensions (round: ((π × radius) × radius) × depth; square or rectangular: (length × width) × depth), rounded to whole millilitres and pinned; Bundt and tube pans use the standard conversion chart's labelled capacity (12 cups = 2844 ml, 16 cups = 3792 ml at 1 cup = 237 ml), not a geometric value. Custom sizes are entered in centimetres and computed from the unrounded volume; the factor is shown to 2 decimal places, the percentage to 1 decimal place, and the scaled quantity to 1 decimal place, each rounded once (half-up), and scaling always uses the unrounded factor. Depth relation compares the two depths: the target is shallower, deeper, or the same; it cannot be compared when one side is a nominal-capacity pan. The downloaded report is always named baking-pan-report.json; its content is the result as compact JSON with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code units and one trailing line feed.

This tool scales one total batter quantity only. It does not scale individual ingredient weights, and it does not calculate baking time or temperature adjustments.

Frequently asked questions

How do I read the factor?
Factor = target pan volume ÷ source pan volume. Above 1 means make more batter; below 1 means make less.
Why do Bundt and tube pans use nominal capacity?
Their shapes (flutes, a centre tube) are not a plain cylinder or box, so no geometric formula gives the real capacity; the labelled capacity from a standard conversion chart is used and marked nominal.
Can it scale each ingredient's weight?
No. This tool scales one total batter quantity only; for ingredient-ratio conversions use the Baker's Percentage Calculator.
