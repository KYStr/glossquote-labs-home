    Method and rules

    Naismith's rule: flat ground at 5 km/h, 12 minutes per kilometre; ascent adds 1 hour per 600 m, 10 minutes per 100 m. Descent follows the Langmuir correction: a gentle descent (slope 5°–12°) saves 10 minutes per 300 m descended, a steep descent (slope over 12°) adds 10 minutes per 300 m; no adjustment ignores descent. The gentle-descent subtraction never makes the total negative; the floor is 0.

    The total is the exact value rounded half-up to whole minutes; the flat, ascent and descent-adjustment segments are each rounded half-up to 1 decimal place, so the three segments do not necessarily add up to the whole-minute total — the total governs. Average pace is the floored total divided by the distance; equivalent flat distance is the distance plus 7.92 × ascent ÷ 1000 km, with descent excluded.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does the estimate include breaks?

    No, this is moving time only; add breaks separately.

    Why does a steep descent take longer?

    The Langmuir correction adds 10 minutes per 300 m on steep descents; only gentle descents save time.
