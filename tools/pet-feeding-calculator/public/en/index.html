How it is calculated, and the input format

Resting energy requirement RER = 70 × (weight in kg)^0.75; the daily requirement DER = RER × a life-stage factor. The factor table comes from the MSD Veterinary Manual table “Daily Maintenance Energy Requirements for Dogs and Cats” (verified 2026-11-09, cross-checked against OSU VMC, the Pet Nutrition Alliance, and Small Animal Clinical Nutrition, 5th ed.). RER and DER are each rounded to whole kcal; daily grams are converted from the rounded DER (DER × 100 ÷ kcal per 100 g) and rounded to whole grams; cups are rounded to two decimals.

Numbers take at most two decimal places; spaces, signs, exponents and leading zeros are not accepted (except 0 itself). Pregnancy, lactation, working dogs, critical care, weight-gain prescriptions and senior pets are out of scope: published guidelines give ranges only for those, with no single pinnable value. The downloaded report is always named pet-feeding-report.json; its content is the result as compact JSON with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code unit and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

What is RER?
Resting energy requirement: RER = 70 × (weight in kg)^0.75, the daily calories a resting pet needs for basic body functions; the daily requirement (DER) multiplies RER by a life-stage factor.
Why does this differ from the feeding guide on the bag?
Bag guides assume an average pet eating that product; this tool estimates from your pet's weight and life stage. Both are starting points — individual needs vary by up to 50%, so adjust by body condition.
Cups or grams — which is more accurate?
Grams are more accurate. Cup amounts vary with kibble size and how the cup is filled; use the kcal-per-cup figure printed on your food's label.
Can I use this for a pregnant or nursing pet?
No. Needs during gestation and lactation vary widely by stage and litter size, and published guidelines give ranges only — ask your veterinarian.
