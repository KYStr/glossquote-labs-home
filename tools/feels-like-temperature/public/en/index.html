Feels-Like Temperature Calculator

Before heading out, enter the air temperature, wind speed and relative humidity: wind chill for cold weather, the heat index for hot weather, each within its official validity domain; when neither applies, the tool honestly reports the actual temperature with no adjustment. A feels-like temperature is a model estimate, not medical or safety advice.

How it is calculated, and the input format

The model is chosen by domain, in a fixed order: wind chill first (the joint Environment Canada / U.S. NWS 2001 formula, valid for air temperature ≤ 10 °C and wind speed > 4.8 km/h — exactly 4.8 does not qualify), then the heat index (the Rothfusz regression plus the two standard NWS adjustments, with this tool's domain of air temperature ≥ 27 °C and relative humidity ≥ 40%). When neither applies, the tool reports the actual temperature itself, flagged as outside both model domains. The Australian apparent temperature is not offered, so one input never yields two competing feels-like numbers.

Rounding is applied exactly once, at output: floor(x×10＋0.5)／10, half-up to one decimal place. Nothing is ever clamped: near its domain corner (27 °C, 40% humidity) the heat index regression can legitimately read about 0.1 °C below the air temperature, and the formula value is reported as-is. Inside its domain, wind chill always reads below the air temperature.

Inputs accept ASCII digits and one decimal point only: the temperature may carry a minus sign, wind speed and humidity may not; plus signs, spaces, exponents and full-width digits are format errors, and the tool never guesses. The downloaded report is always named feels-like-report.json; its content is the result as compact JSON with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code units and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

What happens when neither model applies?
The tool reports the actual temperature, flagged as outside both model domains, with no adjustment.
Why is the wind-chill threshold 4.8 km/h?
It is the official lower bound of the NWS / Environment Canada formula domain (3 mph); the formula assumes wind measured at 10 m height.
Why can the heat index read slightly below the air temperature near its domain edge?
The Rothfusz regression fit near 27 °C and 40% humidity can land about 0.1 °C below the air temperature; the tool reports the formula value and never clamps it.
Why is the Australian apparent temperature not offered?
This tool uses only the two North American official models, so one input never yields two competing feels-like numbers.
