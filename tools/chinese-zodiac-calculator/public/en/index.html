How the sign is decided, and the CNY table

The zodiac year starts at Chinese New Year: a birth on or after that year's CNY takes that year's sign; a birth before it takes the previous year's sign. The CNY day itself already belongs to the new sign.

The tool carries a pinned Chinese New Year table for 1960–2035, plus boundary rows for 1959 and 2036 that settle births at the very start of 1960 and the very end of 2035. Dates outside the input ranges are reported as out of range — nothing is extrapolated.

The stem and branch come from the zodiac year: branch index = (year − 4) mod 12, stem index = (year − 4) mod 10. 1960 is a Geng-Zi Rat year, 1984 a Jia-Zi Rat year and 2024 a Jia-Chen Dragon year, exactly as the formulas give.

FAQ

Why do January and February birthdays so often get the wrong sign?
Because the zodiac year starts at Chinese New Year (lunar 1/1), not on January 1. CNY falls between January 21 and February 20, so anyone born before it still belongs to the previous year's sign.
What is Ben Ming Nian?
When the as-of date falls in a zodiac year with the same sign as your birth year, that is your Ben Ming Nian — it comes around every 12 years. Enter an as-of date and this tool tells you whether it is one, and when the next one starts.
Why might a BaZi chart give a different sign?
BaZi switches the year at Li Chun (around February 4) instead of Chinese New Year. People born between Li Chun and CNY get different signs under the two systems. This tool uses the CNY boundary.
