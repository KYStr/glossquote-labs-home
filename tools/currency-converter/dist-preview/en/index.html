How it calculates, and the formats used

The rate means “units of the to currency per 1 unit of the from currency”. From → To multiplies the amount by the rate; To → From divides the amount by the rate you entered, exactly, rounding once at the end — never by re-multiplying the rounded inverse rate shown on screen. The inverse rate is display-only, at 6 significant digits (not 6 decimal places). The quick table always converts 1, 10, 100 and 1000 units of the from currency into the to currency, each row rounded independently from its exact product, regardless of the selected direction.

All arithmetic is exact integer rational arithmetic (the rate scaled by one million, the amount scaled by one hundred), with half-up rounding (exactly half rounds up), applied once at output. Amounts display with exactly two decimal places, with no thousands separators and no currency symbols. The downloaded report is always named currency-converter-report.json; its content is the result as compact JSON, with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code units and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the rate come from?
From you. This tool never connects to a rate service and ships with no built-in rates; it computes with exactly the rate you enter.
Why is the reverse direction not computed from the displayed inverse rate?
The inverse rate is rounded to 6 significant digits for display only. Reverse conversions always divide by the rate you entered, exactly, and round once at the end, so display rounding never accumulates.
Can I enter any 3 letters as a currency code?
Yes. Codes are not checked against the ISO 4217 list, because the list changes and a stale list would wrongly reject valid codes; the tool assigns no meaning to a code, and using the same code on both sides is allowed.
Will the result match what my bank actually charges?
Not necessarily. Real transactions add fees and a buy/sell spread; enter the rate your bank or provider actually offers you and use the result to check it.
