Formulas and sources

The healthy weight range uses the WHO adult normal band, BMI 18.5–24.9: the lower bound is 18.5 × height (m)² and the upper bound is 24.9 × height (m)². The BMI-22 reference weight is 22 × height (m)², a single-point reference commonly used in Taiwan clinical and health-education practice, not an official cut-off.

All four formulas take 5 feet (152.4 cm) as the base and add a fixed number of kilograms per inch over 5 feet; height converts with 1 in = 2.54 cm, and below 5 feet the formulas extend along the same straight line. Devine (1974): men 50 + 2.3 kg/in, women 45.5 + 2.3 kg/in (Devine BJ. Gentamicin therapy. Drug Intell Clin Pharm. 1974;8:650–655). Robinson (1983): men 52 + 1.9, women 49 + 1.7 (Robinson JD, et al. Am J Hosp Pharm. 1983;40:1016–1019). Miller (1983): men 56.2 + 1.41, women 53.1 + 1.36 (Miller DR, et al. Am J Hosp Pharm. 1983;40:1622–1625). Hamwi (1964) was published in pounds: men 106 lb + 6 lb/in, women 100 lb + 5 lb/in (Hamwi GJ. In: Danowski TS, ed. Diabetes Mellitus: Diagnosis and Treatment. ADA; 1964:73–78); this tool computes in pounds first and converts with the exact 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, rather than the approximate kilogram constants common elsewhere. For an overview of all four, see Pai MP, Paloucek FP. Ann Pharmacother. 2000;34:1066–1069.

Every output is rounded half-up (0.5 rounds up) to one decimal place only at the final step, with no intermediate rounding. The downloaded report is always named ideal-weight-report.json; it holds the result as compact JSON with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code unit and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

Why do the four formulas give different numbers?
They come from different decades and samples and were mostly designed for drug dosing; none is the single right answer, and showing them side by side makes the spread visible.
Is this range the same as Taiwan's HPA standard?
The lower bound matches (BMI 18.5), the upper differs: this page shows the WHO bound 24.9, while Taiwan's HPA healthy band is 18.5 ≤ BMI < 24, giving a lower upper weight.
What if I am shorter than 5 feet?
The formulas extend along the same straight line (the per-inch step is unchanged). They were published for adults around 5 feet and taller, so treat results at shorter heights as rough references.
Why is there no frame-size adjustment?
Only Hamwi's original note suggests ±10% for frame size; the other formulas have none, and no single citable rule applies it across all four, so this tool does not adjust.
Can I use these numbers as a weight-loss target?
No. They are population-level statistical references, not personal medical advice; talk to a physician or registered dietitian about personal goals.
