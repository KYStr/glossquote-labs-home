Ingredient density table (grams per cup) and the volume basis

Ingredient weights per cup are fixed estimates. King Arthur Baking’s Ingredient Weight Chart lists water and fresh milk at 227 g per cup each and honey at 21 g per tablespoon, which gives 336 g per cup using 16 tablespoons per cup. The tool uses 89 g per cup for old-fashioned or quick-cooking oats. Dry goods are spooned and levelled, and brown sugar is packed.

For butter, page 1 of King Arthur Baking’s Easy As Pie gives 227 g per cup of unsalted butter.

The tool’s volume basis is 1 cup = 240 mL, 1 tablespoon = 15 mL, and 1 teaspoon = 5 mL, using the FDA household measures for nutrition labelling. A US customary cup is about 236.588 mL. The model applies the US weight-per-cup figures to its 240 mL cup. The two cup volumes differ by about 1.4%.

Frequently asked questions

How many millilitres is a cup here?
This tool uses the FDA household measure for nutrition labelling: 240 mL per cup. A US customary cup is about 236.588 mL.
Why isn't a cup of water 240 grams?
For volume-to-weight conversions, the tool uses the ingredient chart’s estimate of 227 g per cup of water. For volume-only conversions, 1 cup equals 240 mL.
What if my ingredient is not listed?
Weight-to-volume conversion only works for the 12 table ingredients; for volume-only conversions choose "None".
