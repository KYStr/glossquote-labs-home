How it is calculated, and where the formulas come from

The Fox formula is 220 − age. The Tanaka formula is 208 − 0.7 × age, from Tanaka, Monahan & Seals (2001), “Age-predicted maximal heart rate revisited”, Journal of the American College of Cardiology 37(1). The Tanaka value is first rounded to a whole maximum heart rate, then split into five continuous zones with the shared-boundary +1 rule: each zone starts one bpm above the previous zone's end, and zone 5 always ends at the used maximum.

The Karvonen method multiplies the heart rate reserve (used maximum − resting heart rate) by each band percentage and adds the resting heart rate back; boundaries are rounded the same way and use the same shared-boundary +1 rule. All arithmetic is integer tenths arithmetic with no floating point. This tool has no download or copy button; the results are plain selectable text on the page.

Frequently asked questions

Why two maximum heart-rate formulas?
The Fox formula (220 − age) is older and less accurate; the Tanaka formula (208 − 0.7 × age) comes from newer research. This tool calculates zones with Tanaka and shows Fox for comparison only.
What if I have a measured maximum heart rate?
Enter it as the measured maximum heart rate; it takes precedence over the formula estimates, both zone tables are calculated from it, and age may be left blank.
