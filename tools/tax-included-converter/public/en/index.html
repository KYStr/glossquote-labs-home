How it is calculated, and the input format

Net to gross: tax = net × rate, rounded half-up to the dollar; gross = net + tax. Gross to net: net = gross ÷ (1 + rate), rounded half-up to the dollar; tax = gross − net, so the single rounding is absorbed by the tax and the three figures always balance. Multiple lines: all net lines are summed into the sales amount first, then the tax is computed once on that total; taxing each line separately and adding can differ by NT$1, and the difference is shown. The 5% default is the current levy (the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, Article 10, sets a 5–10% range with the rate set by the Executive Yuan; the Ministry of Finance states the current rate is 5%); Article 14 is the rounding rule.

Amounts are whole New Taiwan dollars only: no decimals, thousands commas, signs, spaces or exponents; the maximum is NT$999,999,999,999, and the lines subtotal shares that cap. The rate is the only field that may carry decimals — at most two, above 0% and up to 100%. At most 20 lines. The downloaded report is always named tax-included-converter-report.json; its contents are the result as compact JSON with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code units and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this result to issue an invoice directly?
No. This is an estimate for quoting and filling in an invoice; issuing a uniform or e-invoice and filing must follow the tax rules.
Why does the per-line tax sum differ from the tax on the total?
Because the half-up rounding to the dollar happens only once. Rounding each line separately and adding can differ from rounding the total once by NT$1; the invoice structure taxes the total once, so this tool uses the total and shows the difference.
Why is the default rate 5%?
5% is the current Taiwan business tax levy (the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, Article 10, sets a 5–10% range with the rate set by the Executive Yuan). Enter another rate for other situations.
