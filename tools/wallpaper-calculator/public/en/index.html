How it is calculated, and the input format

The order is fixed: each wall's effective width is divided by the roll width and rounded up to get its strip count; each strip's length is the wall height plus a fixed 10 cm trim allowance (5 cm at the top and bottom), rounded up to whole pattern repeats; drops per roll are the roll length divided by the strip length, rounded down; the waste percentage inflates each wall's strip count (rounded up) before that wall's rolls are counted. Leftovers are never shared between walls — per-wall rolls are counted separately and then added.

Openings semantics: a strip is one full floor-to-ceiling length. In dimension-list mode, opening widths are deducted from the perimeter — horizontal positions fully covered by an opening need no strip — but strip length never shortens, and the material above and below an opening is conservatively treated as used. With a total area only, no opening position is known, so strips and rolls are unchanged and only the reported net area is reduced. Areas are computed exactly in square centimetres, then rounded half up to 0.01 m² for display; they are for reference only and never feed the strip or roll counts (beyond the area deduction itself).

Inputs accept ASCII digits and one decimal point only: metres allow at most two decimal places, and the pattern repeat and waste percent are whole numbers; commas, spaces, signs and full-width digits are format errors, and the tool never guesses. The downloaded report is always named wallpaper-report.json; its content is the result as compact JSON with object keys sorted recursively by UTF-16 code units and one trailing newline.

Frequently asked questions

Why doesn't the openings area always reduce the roll count?
Strips are counted by width: dimension-list openings remove the strips they fully cover, while an area-only figure has no position, so it only reduces the reported net area.
Why does each strip get 10 cm extra?
A fixed 5 cm trim allowance at the top and bottom, then the length is rounded up to whole pattern repeats.
Where does the waste percentage apply?
It inflates each wall's strip count (rounded up) before rolls are counted; leftovers are not shared between walls.
