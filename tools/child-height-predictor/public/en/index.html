This is a statistical estimate based on the parents' heights. It is not medical advice and is not a diagnosis or guarantee for any individual child. Genetics is only one of many factors that influence adult height; nutrition, overall health, sleep and the timing of puberty also matter, and an individual child may well finish outside the estimated range.

Rules and limits
  • Formula: boy (father + mother + 13) ÷ 2, girl (father + mother − 13) ÷ 2, in cm; the target range is the estimate ±8.5 cm.
  • Height format: a number in cm with at most one decimal place, such as 178 or 172.5. No thousands commas, exponents, signs, or leading zeros.
  • Each field holds at most 8 characters; the accepted range is 100.0–230.0 cm. Results are rounded half-up to one decimal place, once only.
  • The child's age or current height is not needed; this method uses only the parents' heights.
  • Data stays in the current page draft only; leaving or switching language discards it. Nothing is stored or uploaded.

FAQ

What does the range mean?

The ±8.5 cm target range spans roughly the 3rd to 97th percentile; most children finish inside it, but some finish outside it.

Why add 13 cm for a boy and subtract it for a girl?

13 cm is the conventional average adult height difference between men and women; it puts both parents' heights on the child's scale before averaging.

Do I need my child's current height or age?

No. This method uses only the two parents' heights. Tracking growth percentiles from a child's current height is out of scope.

Whose heights should I enter?

The biological parents' adult heights; barefoot measured values are best.
