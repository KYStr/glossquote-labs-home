Rules and limits
  • Waist 20 to 300 cm, height 50 to 300 cm. Numbers are decimals with at most 2 decimal places; no thousands commas, exponents, signs or spaces. Each field holds at most 16 characters.
  • The displayed ratio is rounded to 2 decimals. The category uses the exact unrounded ratio: below 0.40 is below the healthy range, 0.40 to under 0.50 is healthy, 0.50 to under 0.60 is increased central adiposity, and 0.60 or above is high central adiposity.
  • The healthy waist range is 0.40 to 0.50 times your height, to 1 decimal place. The distance to target is shown only when the exact ratio is 0.50 or above: waist minus half your height, to 1 decimal place.
  • The categories apply to adults with a BMI under 35 kg/m², with the same cut-offs for men and women and all ethnicities. At a BMI of 35 or above, waist adds little extra prediction of risk, so this tool makes no extra adjustment and the result remains for reference only.
  • How to measure: stand relaxed and measure at the end of a normal breath out, with the tape horizontal at the midpoint between the lower edge of the lowest rib and the top of the hip bone (iliac crest), snug but not compressing the skin (WHO STEPS method).
  • Data stays in the current page draft only; leaving or switching language discards it. Nothing is stored or uploaded.

FAQ

Why does it show 0.50 but say healthy?

The category uses the exact unrounded ratio; the display is rounded to 2 decimals. For example, 84.9 ÷ 170 = 0.4994…, which displays as 0.50 but is still healthy.

How do I measure my waist?

Stand relaxed and measure at the end of a normal breath out, with the tape horizontal at the midpoint between the lower edge of the lowest rib and the top of the hip bone, snug but not compressing the skin.

How is WHtR different from BMI?

BMI uses weight and height and reflects overall body weight; WHtR uses waist and height and reflects abdominal fat distribution. They complement each other and cannot replace one another.
