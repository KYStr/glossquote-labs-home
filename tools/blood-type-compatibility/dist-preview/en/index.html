How it works

Red-cell compatibility follows the antigen rule: the donor's red-cell antigen set (O is empty, A is {A}, B is {B}, AB is {A,B}, plus D when Rh positive) must be a subset of the recipient's. Receive-from lists are derived by transposing the same 8×8 matrix, never from a second table. Plasma runs the other way: the recipient's ABO antigen set must be a subset of the donor's, Rh is ignored, and lists are expanded to all eight types with both Rh variants of each ABO group.

Frequently asked questions

Why is O negative the universal red-cell donor?
O red cells carry neither A nor B antigens, and Rh negative cells carry no D antigen, so no recipient's antibodies attack them; O negative can give to all eight blood types.
Why is AB the universal plasma donor instead?
Plasma compatibility turns on the donor plasma's antibodies: AB plasma has neither anti-A nor anti-B antibodies, so it can be given to every ABO group, while O plasma has both and can go only to O recipients. Plasma compatibility does not depend on the Rh factor.
