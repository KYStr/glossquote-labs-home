Data source, limits and FAQ

Storage times are the literal values of the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service FoodKeeper, checked item by item against FoodKeeper item numbers for all 30 foods; weeks, months and years convert to 7, 30 and 365 days. Fridge times for meat, eggs and leftovers were cross-checked against the FoodSafety.gov Cold Food Storage Chart. Table checked 2026-11-21. Freezer time is a quality limit: food kept at or below −18°C the whole time stays safe past its freezer time, but flavour and texture decline; past a fridge time, throw it out.

This tool only derives a storage limit from dates. It does not judge whether food is actually spoiled and is not medical or nutrition advice. When in doubt, throw it out — do not judge by look or smell.

FAQ

How is the safe-until date calculated?

The start date plus the lower end of the storage-time range for that food and location; the upper end is the quality limit.

Is food safe after its freezer time?

If kept at or below −18°C the whole time it stays safe past the freezer time, but quality drops; past a fridge time, throw it out.

Why does milk have no fridge days?

FoodKeeper defers to the use-by date on the package for refrigerated milk; enter that date.
