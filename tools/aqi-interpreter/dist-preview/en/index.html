    How levels are assigned and the fixed rules

    Six levels (shared by both systems): 0–50 Good (green #00E400), 51–100 Moderate (yellow #FFFF00), 101–150 Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (orange #FF7E00), 151–200 Unhealthy (red #FF0000), 201–300 Very Unhealthy (purple #99004C), 301–500 Hazardous (maroon #7E0023). Concentrations are converted by piecewise linear interpolation, sub-indices are rounded half-up to integers, and the largest sub-index is the AQI; ties are broken by the fixed pollutant order PM2.5, PM10, O3 8-hour, O3 1-hour, CO, SO2, NO2.

    Taiwan and US EPA share the levels; the concentration breakpoints differ. For example, the Good limit for PM2.5 is 12.4 µg/m³ in Taiwan and 9.0 µg/m³ in the US; for PM10 it is 30 µg/m³ in Taiwan and 54 µg/m³ in the US; the 1-hour O3 sensitive band is 0.101–0.134 ppm in Taiwan and 0.125–0.164 ppm in the US. Rounding also differs: Taiwan rounds half-up, the US truncates.

    When 8-hour O3 exceeds 0.200 ppm, the official rule computes AQI values above 300 from 1-hour O3 instead, so enter the 1-hour O3 concentration. When 1-hour SO2 exceeds 304 ppb, the official rule uses the 24-hour SO2 value for AQI values above 200; this tool only accepts the 1-hour value, so that row is undefined. Other pollutants above the table top are capped at a sub-index of 500.

    PM2.5/PM10 breakpoints are defined on 24-hour averages, O3/CO on 8-hour or 1-hour averages, and SO2/NO2 on 1-hour averages; enter concentrations on the stated averaging basis, as this tool does not compute moving averages. All computation happens locally on this page; nothing is uploaded, stored, or logged.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why does the same concentration give a different AQI in Taiwan vs the US?
    Because the breakpoint tables differ (for example, the Good limit for PM2.5 is 12.4 µg/m³ in Taiwan and 9.0 µg/m³ in the US); see the comparison table in SPEC §5.
    Does this tool forecast tomorrow's air quality?
    No. It only interprets the numbers you enter; it does not forecast or fetch live monitoring values.
    Can I enter a real-time reading as a 24-hour average?
    No. PM2.5/PM10 breakpoints are defined on 24-hour averages and O3/CO on 8-hour or 1-hour averages; a real-time reading is indicative only, and this tool does not compute moving averages.
    Why is there no result when 8-hour O3 exceeds 0.200 ppm?
    By the official rule, AQI values above 300 are computed from 1-hour O3; enter the 1-hour O3 concentration instead.
