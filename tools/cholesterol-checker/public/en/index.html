Classification rules and limits
  • All four fields are whole numbers in mg/dL, from 1 to 9999; each field holds at most 5 characters. No decimals, signs, spaces, or exponents.
  • Classification follows the NCEP ATP III table: intervals are left-closed, so a cut-point value belongs to the higher band (for example, total cholesterol 200 is borderline high, LDL 100 is near or above optimal).
  • Non-HDL cholesterol = total cholesterol − HDL, for reference only and not classified; when total cholesterol is not greater than HDL, the integer is still shown as-is.
  • The overall summary takes the most severe of the four bands: all desirable, borderline when the worst is a borderline value, and needs attention when any value needs attention.
  • Data stays in the current page draft only; leaving or switching language discards it. Nothing is stored or uploaded.

FAQ

Which standard is used?

NCEP ATP III (U.S. National Cholesterol Education Program); values are in mg/dL.

Why is high HDL good?

ATP III treats HDL ≥60 mg/dL as a “negative” risk factor — protective; low HDL (<40) is the concern.

Do I need to fast?

The ATP III classification assumes a 9–12 hour fasting lipid profile; triglycerides are most affected by eating.

Is this a diagnosis?

No. This tool is for reference only and is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.
