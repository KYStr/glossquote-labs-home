Calculation rules and limits
  • Appliance list of 1 to 12 rows; each name 1 to 40 characters, watts are whole numbers, running watts 1 to 20000 W, starting watts 1 to 30000 W, and starting watts must not be below running watts. Watts accept no decimals, symbols, spaces or leading zeros (007 is invalid).
  • Total running watts is the sum of running watts; the starting surge adds only the largest single starting jump (starting minus running watts) — motors start one at a time, never together.
  • Running watts with margin is total running watts × 1.25, rounded up to whole watts; kVA is watts ÷ 0.8 ÷ 1000, rounded up to 0.1 kVA; recommended kVA is the larger of required kVA and surge kVA. Surge kVA carries no extra 25% margin.
  • The retail size table is 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0, 5.0, 6.0, 7.5, 10.0, 12.5, 15.0, 20.0 kVA; the smallest size at or above the recommended kVA is used. Above 20.0 kVA the size is rounded up to the nearest 0.5 kVA and flagged as oversize.
  • The preset watts table comes from the Honda official wattage estimation guide, Home Applications table, with ranges taken at their upper bound. These are approximate values, not nameplate values; your appliances' nameplate values always win.
  • Data stays only in this page's draft; leaving the page or switching languages discards it. Nothing is stored or uploaded.

FAQ

Why add only the largest starting watts?

When motors start one at a time, only one is starting at any moment; starting several motors at once is out of scope — stagger the starts.

What are the 25% safety margin and 0.8 power factor?

The margin keeps continuous load at or below 80% of the rated capacity; 0.8 is the standard nameplate power factor for generators, kVA = kW ÷ 0.8. Both are fixed values pinned by this tool.

Does this replace a professional electrical assessment?

No, it is a sizing estimate from nameplate approximations; confirm wiring, start order and capacity against the appliance and generator nameplates and a professional.
