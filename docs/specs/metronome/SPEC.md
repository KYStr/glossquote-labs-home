# WebAudio Metronome with Tap Tempo — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. The one-sentence contract: every click time is computed from the WebAudio clock and scheduled ahead; the UI timer exists only to trigger scheduling, never to keep time.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: music students, teachers, and practising musicians (including, in this catalogue's home market, the many piano/guitar students who currently use ad-heavy phone apps). The recurring problem: naive browser metronomes schedule each click from a JavaScript timer, so tempo drifts and stutters whenever the main thread is busy — exactly when a practice tool must be boring and exact. Success is observable: the user sets 30–240 BPM (slider, number, or tap), chooses a time signature and subdivision, presses start, and hears a click track whose beat intervals stay within the stated tolerance of the audio clock for minutes at a time, with beat 1 accented and the current beat shown; tempo changes land cleanly on the next beat. Non-goals: recording, playback of songs, tempo maps/ritardando, MIDI or external sync, practice logging/accounts, and any promise of sound while the browser has suspended the tab (stated honestly in the UI).

Assumptions: a modern desktop browser with WebAudio; sound on; a user gesture starts audio (browser policy — the start button is that gesture).

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) the lookahead scheduler of section 4 (the product's core); (b) tempo: integer BPM 30–240 via slider, numeric input, ± buttons, and tap tempo per section 4's tap rules; (c) time signatures: beats per bar 2–12 with beat unit 4 (quarter) or 8 (eighth) from a fixed list — (2,4),(3,4),(4,4),(5,4),(6,4),(7,4),(2,8),(3,8),(6,8),(9,8),(12,8) — display as e.g. "6/8"; (d) subdivisions per beat: 1, 2, 3 (triplet), 4, with subdivision clicks quieter than beat clicks; (e) accent toggle for beat 1 (default on); (f) volume 0–100%; (g) visual beat indicator: bar progress with beat numbers, driven from the schedule (section 4), plus a flashing beat dot that follows the audio clock; (h) start/stop, with stop silencing future scheduled clicks immediately. Excluded: swing, polyrhythms, multiple simultaneous tempi, preset songs, saving settings (session only), background operation guarantees, and audio files of any kind (all sounds are synthesised in code).
Distinction from existing work: Focus Timer is a silent visual countdown; the TTS Reader plays speech, not a scheduled click track, and its contract is about utterance queues, not clock accuracy. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time implements a metronome or an audio scheduler; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: stopped; BPM 100, signature 4/4, subdivision 1, accent on, volume 80%. The page explains in one line that timing is generated by the device's audio clock and that switching away may pause sound in some browsers.
Start: pressing Start creates/resumes the AudioContext inside the click handler, records the start time per section 4, and begins scheduling; the button becomes Stop. While running, BPM/signature/subdivision/accent/volume controls remain live (changes apply per section 4's change rules); the beat indicator shows the current bar and beat, and the subdivision ticks are visually subdued.
Stop: cancels the scheduler timer, silences already-scheduled-but-unplayed clicks (per section 4), resets the beat counter to bar 1 beat 1 for the next start, and returns controls to the stopped state. Natural page events: when the document becomes hidden, the page shows (on return) a notice that the browser may have paused audio while hidden — it does not claim the track continued; the scheduler's behaviour on return is defined in section 4 (it resumes from the audio clock without a burst of catch-up clicks).
Settings live in memory only; reload restores the defaults above. Keyboard: all controls are native and reachable; Space on the Start/Stop button works as a button (the page does not hijack global Space). A "flash screen on beat 1" is NOT a feature (photosensitivity) — the beat indicator is a small local element, and a reduced-flash setting is unnecessary because no full-screen flashing exists.

## 4. Scheduler, timing rules and exact behaviour
Scheduler (normative): the controller runs a lookahead scheduler with timer interval 25 ms and schedule horizon 0.12 s. State: `nextNoteTime` (audio-clock seconds) and `currentBeat` (0-based within the bar) plus `currentSubdivision`. While running, each scheduler tick schedules every event with time < `context.currentTime + 0.12`, advancing by the current event spacing. Beat spacing = 60 / BPM seconds; subdivision spacing = beat spacing / subdivisions. Event times are derived only from the audio clock arithmetic — the 25 ms timer's own jitter MUST NOT enter event times (a test jitters the timer and verifies scheduled times are unchanged, section 7).
Click synthesis (normative ranges): each click is an oscillator burst (square or sine) with an exponential-decay gain envelope, total duration ≤ 50 ms, no audio assets. Frequencies: accent 1568 Hz, normal beat 1046 Hz, subdivision 784 Hz (these are the product's fixed choices; relative ordering accent > beat > subdivision is the contract, exact values are stated here so tests can assert them). Gains: accent 1.0 × volume, beat 0.7 × volume, subdivision 0.4 × volume, applied through a master gain node. Two clicks never overlap audibly at the maximum tempo/subdivision (240 BPM × 4 = 62.5 ms spacing > 50 ms click — this inequality is why the click duration cap is 50 ms).
Start time: on Start, `nextNoteTime = context.currentTime + 0.06` and the first event is beat 1 of bar 1. Stop: clear the timer and disconnect/silence the master gain (or cancel scheduled sources) so no already-scheduled click sounds after Stop is pressed (implementations typically track scheduled sources and stop them; the observable requirement is silence within 100 ms of the press).
Change rules (normative): BPM, signature and subdivision changes made while running apply to events not yet scheduled; events already inside the 0.12 s horizon keep their times (the horizon is short enough that this is at most one beat at slow tempi). Formally: the scheduler reads tempo/signature/subdivision at the moment it computes each advance, so a change takes effect at the first event scheduled after the change. Changing beats-per-bar resets the beat counter to beat 1 at the next bar boundary as computed by the scheduler (if the new bar length is shorter than the current beat index, the next event starts a new bar). Tap tempo: taps are timestamped with the audio clock (or performance.now consistently); a tap sequence is a run of taps each within 2.0 s of the previous (a longer gap restarts the run); from ≥2 taps, BPM = round(60 / mean interval of the last up to 6 intervals), clamped to 30–240; the computed value is applied as a BPM change (and while running, follows the change rules). A single tap does nothing but start a run.
Visual sync: the beat indicator is drawn from a requestAnimationFrame loop that reads `context.currentTime` and the schedule's event list to find the latest event whose time ≤ now — it never counts frames or timer ticks. If the tab was hidden and the audio clock advanced, on return the scheduler does not emit catch-up events: events whose computed time is in the past at scheduling time are skipped (their beat positions still advance the counter) until `nextNoteTime` is again within the horizon — the audible result is the track resuming in phase, possibly mid-bar, and the indicator shows the true position.
Accuracy contract: with a steady tempo, scheduled event times deviate from the ideal grid (start + k × spacing) by 0 in the model (they are computed additively from the previous scheduled time; implementations MUST accumulate `nextNoteTime += spacing` rather than recomputing from `currentTime`, which would import timer jitter). The real-browser evidence (section 7) measures rendered click onsets against the grid with a tolerance of ±10 ms over 60 seconds at 120 BPM including a busy-main-thread stress interval — this tolerance covers platform audio latency variation, not scheduler error.

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (tempo/tap computations, signature/subdivision tables, scheduler state machine over an injected clock and timer) with zero DOM/WebAudio access; a WebAudio adapter (context, click synthesis, source bookkeeping); a controller; a thin DOM layer. Runtime dependencies: none. The core scheduler is fully unit-testable against a fake clock — that is a design requirement, not a nicety, because the accuracy contract lives there.
Privacy/security: no network, no storage, no microphone or any permission; audio is generated and played locally; nothing is recorded. Volume defaults are hearing-safe (master gain capped at 1.0; no sudden full-scale onset — the first click is at least 60 ms after the gesture).
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI; controls are native sliders/inputs/selects/buttons with labels and value readouts (BPM value is text, not slider position alone); the beat indicator is supplementary — a text line states "Bar 2 · Beat 3" for the current position during play; start/stop state is announced politely. The tool is inherently audio, so the visual/text position display is the accessibility floor, stated as such.
Performance bounds: scheduler work per tick is O(events in horizon) ≤ a handful; rAF indicator is one DOM update per frame; a 10-minute run at 240 BPM × 4 subdivisions (38,400 events) MUST NOT accumulate unbounded state (scheduled-source bookkeeping is pruned as events pass). Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs from a static host — note file:// may restrict AudioContext in some browsers, so the preview server is the reference environment.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages; all signatures/subdivisions; tap tempo; accent toggle; change-while-running per the change rules; stop silences within 100 ms.
- boundaries: BPM 30 and 240 (slider beyond is impossible; typed out-of-range rejected or clamped per the input rule — clamped with the value shown, never silently different); subdivision 4 at 240 BPM click non-overlap; tap gaps at exactly 2.0 s.
- privacy: zero network after load; no storage; no permissions requested; nothing recorded.
- security: no external resources or audio assets; no eval/Function; all parameters validated before reaching WebAudio calls.
- usability: a musician sets 6/8 with triplet subdivision and starts within seconds; tap tempo lands within ±2 BPM of a steadily tapped reference in a live trial; the hidden-tab notice sets the right expectation.
- accessibility: labelled controls with text values, text position readout, polite state announcements, keyboard-complete operation.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent; correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output from a clean checkout on Node 24; the fake-clock scheduler suite is part of the shipped tests.
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop; the accuracy evidence (below) is produced on at least one real desktop browser; mobile browsers are explicitly out of the scored environment.

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests (fake clock/timer, independently derived expectations): (a) scheduler grid: at 120 BPM the first 20 beat times are exactly start + k×0.5; at 30 BPM × subdivision 4 the spacing is 0.5 s; jittered timer ticks (simulated ±20 ms) produce identical event times; (b) change rules: BPM change mid-run alters only events scheduled after the change; signature change to a shorter bar restarts at beat 1 per the rule; (c) tap tempo: intervals [500, 500, 500] ms → 120 BPM; a 2.1 s gap restarts the run; 7 taps use only the last 6 intervals; clamping at 30/240; (d) stop: no event is scheduled after stop, and scheduled sources are cancelled; (e) hidden-return: events in the past are skipped without catch-up bursts while the beat counter advances correctly; (f) volume/accent gain mapping table.
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): a 60-second run at 120 BPM with the output captured (MediaStreamDestination or an AnalyserNode-based onset log) showing onsets within ±10 ms of the grid, including a 5-second main-thread stress interval; the visual indicator's beat text matches the schedule at sampled moments; keyboard-only start/stop/change flow. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
Explicitly NOT_RUN at specification stage: all product tests above; this document claims document completeness only.
