# WebAudio Metronome with Tap Tempo — preparatory BRIEF v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. Full normative contract is SPEC.md.

## 1. Goal
Musicians practising alone need a metronome that stays accurate over minutes, accents the downbeat, and subdivides the beat — and phone/browser metronomes are notorious for drifting when they schedule sounds naively from a UI timer. Success: the user sets a tempo (30–240 BPM) by slider, number or tapping, picks a time signature (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 6/8 and others from the stated list), optionally subdivides each beat (eighths, triplets, sixteenths), and hears a sample-accurate click track scheduled ahead on the WebAudio clock with the current beat shown visually — the audio keeps time even if the display janks, which the SPEC makes a testable architectural rule rather than a hope.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
WebAudio lookahead scheduling is the normative core: a lookahead scheduler (stated interval and horizon, e.g. checking every 25 ms and scheduling 0.1 s ahead on `AudioContext.currentTime`) with all beat times derived from the audio clock, never from setInterval counts. Controls: BPM 30–240 (integer), tap tempo (average of the stated tap window, with reset after a pause), time signatures with beats-per-bar 2–12 and beat unit 2/4/8, accent on beat 1 (toggleable), subdivisions 1/2/3/4 per beat with quieter non-accent clicks, volume control, and start/stop. Tempo or signature changes take effect at the next beat boundary — the SPEC fixes exactly when a change applies and what happens to an already-scheduled window. Sounds are synthesised oscillator/noise clicks generated in code (no audio files, no downloads); two timbres (accent vs normal vs subdivision) distinguished by pitch, and the visual beat indicator is driven from the same schedule (drawn via the audio clock, not a parallel timer). Excluded: recording, song/tempo maps, ritardando automation, MIDI, background playback promises (the page states honestly that browser tab throttling may suspend audio when hidden — the scheduler design minimises but cannot abolish platform policy), and any storage except — explicitly — nothing: settings are session-only.

Existing tools: Focus Timer is a visual countdown for work; nothing in the catalog produces scheduled audio or measures audio-clock accuracy. Initial catalog/spec/task scope contains no metronome; fresh registry/path/draft check remains mandatory before admission. Timing fixtures are defined against a mock AudioContext clock in unit tests plus a real-browser measurement method (recorded output or AnalyserNode timestamps) for the production evidence, with a stated tolerance.
