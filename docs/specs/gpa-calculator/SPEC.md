# GPA Calculator — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. GPA arithmetic invites two quiet errors this specification forbids: computing in binary floating point and rounding by whatever the runtime defaults to (so 3.325 can surface as 3.32), and "solving" for a target by presenting an impossible required average above the scale maximum as if it were a plan. Here all points are tenths and all credits are halves, so the core computes in integers and every result is exact until one final, stated rounding; and the solver has three named outcomes — feasible, unreachable, guaranteed — of which only the first is a number to aim at.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: secondary and university students checking a term's results, students on a scholarship or honours threshold planning the next term, and advisors doing quick what-if arithmetic with a student. The recurring problem: GPA is a credit-weighted average of grade points, but students intuitively average the letter grades or the per-course percentages, and a target like "3.2 by graduation" hides an algebra step (the required average over *future* credits) that is easy to get wrong and painful to discover late. Success is observable in two panels: the course panel turns a list of grades and credits into the exact weighted GPA on the chosen scale; the solver panel turns completed credits/GPA, planned credits and a target into either the required future average or an honest unreachable/guaranteed statement with the best attainable final GPA. Non-goals: modelling any institution's retake, forgiveness or honours rules, converting percentage scores, or predicting admissions — the page states the plain weighted rule it implements.

Assumptions: a modern desktop or mobile browser; the user's institution uses one of the two shipped scales or close enough that the plain rule is the estimate they want; no network at runtime; nothing is stored.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) a scale selector with exactly two tables, shipped as auditable data in section 4 (4.0 and 4.3); switching scale re-maps every row's grade to the same letter where it exists (A+ exists only on 4.3 — switching from 4.3 to 4.0 with an A+ row is an error state for that row until the user picks another grade, never a silent remap); (b) up to 50 course rows, each: optional label text, letter grade from the active table (a select, not free text), credits in 0.5 steps from 0.5 to 30, and an exclude toggle; (c) outputs for the course panel: total included credits, total grade points, and the GPA under section 4's rounding; (d) the solver panel of section 4 with its three outcomes and the maximum attainable final GPA in the unreachable case; (e) row add/remove and a clear-all. Excluded: percentage entry or percentage→letter conversion tables; saving, accounts or transcript import; retake/forgiveness policies; class rank, honours prediction or any institutional claim.
Distinction from existing work: Grade Target （成績目標試算） answers "what score do I need on this course's remaining assessment to finish the course at X" — one course, in score units, before the course ends. This tool aggregates *finished* courses across a term or a degree, in grade points weighted by credits, and its solver plans over a block of future credits, not one assessment. The inputs, aggregation level and outputs share nothing beyond the word "grade". Running Pace and similar calculators share only generic form arithmetic. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time computes a GPA or a target-GPA solution; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: 4.0 scale, three empty rows (grade unselected, credits blank), GPA panel showing "add at least one included course" instead of a number, solver panel empty with its own prompt. Selecting a grade and credits for a row recomputes all outputs together; rows with an unselected grade or blank credits are incomplete: they are listed with an inline note and excluded from the totals (not silently counted as F or as zero credits), and the totals area states how many incomplete rows are being ignored. The exclude toggle greys the row and removes it from totals while keeping it visible. Removing a row deletes it; a minimum of one row always remains. Switching scale preserves labels and credits; an A+ row under a switch to 4.0 becomes an error row (highlighted, excluded, with the note "A+ is not on the 4.0 scale — pick a grade") until fixed. Solver flow: the user enters completed credits and current GPA, planned credits and target GPA; the outcome panel shows exactly one of the three section 4 outcomes. Any out-of-range solver input marks that field and freezes the outcome at its last valid state labelled out of date. Nothing persists across reload; no figure appears in the URL or title.

## 4. Computation rules (normative)
Scale tables (normative data; MUST ship once as data and match this section exactly):
- 4.0 scale: A 4.0, A− 3.7, B+ 3.3, B 3.0, B− 2.7, C+ 2.3, C 2.0, C− 1.7, D+ 1.3, D 1.0, D− 0.7, F 0.0. Maximum = 4.0.
- 4.3 scale: A+ 4.3, A 4.0, A− 3.7, B+ 3.3, B 3.0, B− 2.7, C+ 2.3, C 2.0, C− 1.7, D+ 1.3, D 1.0, D− 0.7, F 0.0. Maximum = 4.3.
Exact arithmetic: represent points as integer tenths p10 and credits as integer halves c2. For included complete rows: totalPoints×credits numerator N = Σ p10 × c2; denominator D = Σ c2. GPA_exact = N ÷ (10 × D) as a rational. Display: round half-up (ties away from zero; all values are non-negative) to two decimals, applied once. No intermediate rounding of per-course products is needed or permitted (they are integers). F contributes 0 points and its credits DO count in D. Total included credits display = D ÷ 2 with one decimal when odd halves occur (e.g. 7.5), otherwise as an integer.
Credits bounds: per row 0.5–30 in 0.5 steps (c2 ∈ 1..60); rows 1–50; a 51st row is refused with a visible note.
Solver: inputs C0 completed credits (0–1,000, 0.5 steps), G0 current GPA (0 to scale maximum, two decimals), C1 planned credits (0.5–300, 0.5 steps), T target GPA (0 to scale maximum, two decimals). Required average R = (T×(C0+C1) − G0×C0) ÷ C1, computed exactly (all inputs are hundredths or halves — the core uses integer arithmetic in ten-thousandths). Outcomes, in this precedence: if R ≤ 0 → guaranteed: the outcome states the target is already secured even if all planned credits earned 0 points, and shows the final GPA if planned credits earn 0 (= G0×C0 ÷ (C0+C1), same rounding). Else if R > scale maximum → unreachable: the outcome states the target cannot be reached on this scale, shows R (so the user sees how far off it is) and the maximum attainable final GPA = (G0×C0 + max×C1) ÷ (C0+C1) under the same rounding. Else → feasible: show R rounded half-up to two decimals as the required average grade points over the planned credits. Edge: C0 = 0 makes G0 irrelevant (it MUST be ignored, and the field may be left blank only in that case); R then equals T.
Fixtures (independently derived; implementations MUST reproduce exactly):
- P1: 4.0 scale; A (3 cr), B+ (4 cr), C (2 cr) → points 12.0 + 13.2 + 4.0 = 29.2 over 9 credits → 3.2444… → displays 3.24.
- P2: 4.3 scale; A+ (3), A− (3) → 12.9 + 11.1 = 24.0 over 6 → 4.00.
- P3: 4.0 scale; A (3), F (3) → 12.0 over 6 → 2.00 (F credits count).
- P4 (rounding): 4.3 scale; A+ (1), B (3) → 13.3 over 4 = 3.325 exactly → displays 3.33 (half-up; truncation or banker's rounding gives 3.32 and is a defect).
- P5 (solver feasible): C0 60, G0 3.00, C1 30, T 3.20 on 4.0 → R = (288 − 180) ÷ 30 = 3.60 → feasible, required 3.60.
- P6 (solver unreachable): C0 90, G0 2.50, C1 15, T 3.00 on 4.3 → R = (315 − 225) ÷ 15 = 6.00 → unreachable; maximum attainable = (225 + 64.5) ÷ 105 = 2.7571… → 2.76.
- P7 (solver guaranteed): C0 90, G0 3.50, C1 15, T 2.80 → R = (294 − 315) ÷ 15 = −1.40 → guaranteed; final GPA at zero future points = 315 ÷ 105 = 3.00.

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (table lookup, exact integer aggregation, solver with its outcome classification, display rounding) with zero DOM access; a controller; a thin DOM layer. The two scale tables and all bounds appear once as auditable data matching section 4. Runtime dependencies: none.
Privacy/security: no network, no storage, no analytics; course labels are the only free text and render as escaped text only; grades come from selects, never parsed from text; no eval/Function; no external resources.
Honesty rules (normative, testable): the page states that institutions compute GPA differently (retakes, forgiveness, plus/minus variants) and that this tool implements exactly the tables in section 4; an unreachable target is labelled unreachable — the required figure above the scale maximum MUST NOT be presented as an attainable goal; the guaranteed outcome is stated with its condition (zero future points). No honours, scholarship or admission outcome is promised or implied.
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI, including the scale tables' presentation, outcome sentences and the institutional-variation note. Native labelled inputs and selects; the outcome is text, not colour alone; incomplete/error rows are identified by text; keyboard-complete flow including row add/remove and the exclude toggles. Performance bounds: 50 rows of integer arithmetic per edit. Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs offline from file:// or any static host.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages and both scales; fixtures P1–P7 reproduced exactly, including the P4 rounding case and all three solver outcomes with their auxiliary figures.
- boundaries: credits 0.5 and 30 accepted per row, 0 and 30.5 refused; 50 rows accepted, 51st refused; solver C0 0 with blank G0 accepted and R = T; target equal to scale maximum handled; an F row's credits counted.
- privacy: zero network after load; no storage; labels and figures absent from URL/title and never transmitted.
- security: a label containing markup renders inert; no external resources; no eval/Function.
- usability: a student enters five courses and reads their GPA without instructions, and can say whether a stated target is feasible and what average it needs (spot-check).
- accessibility: labelled native controls; outcomes and row states conveyed in text, not colour alone; keyboard-complete including toggles and row management.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent (including outcome sentences and the variation note); correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output on Node 24; the scale tables and bounds appear once as auditable data matching section 4 (a build check compares the shipped tables against the section's values); Node 24.
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop and mobile-width layout; fixtures give identical results across browsers.

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests with independently derived expectations: (a) fixtures P1–P7 exactly, every displayed figure; (b) exactness property: random row sets computed by the core equal a rational-arithmetic reference, and no result differs from the reference's half-up rounding — with a deliberate case at a. xx5 boundary in the style of P4; (c) incomplete and excluded rows: neither affects totals, the incomplete count is reported, an F row affects totals; (d) scale switching: A+ row error state, other rows preserved; (e) solver classification boundaries: R exactly 0 → guaranteed, R exactly scale maximum → feasible, R one hundredth above → unreachable; C0 = 0 behaviour; (f) bounds: every section 4 limit at and beyond.
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): entering P1's courses yields 3.24; switching scales mid-list produces the A+ error state; the solver's three outcomes each displayed from their fixture inputs. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
