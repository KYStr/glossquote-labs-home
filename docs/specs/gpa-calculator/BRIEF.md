# GPA Calculator — preparatory BRIEF v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. Full normative contract is SPEC.md.

## 1. Goal
At the end of every term a student asks two questions: "what is my GPA for these courses" and "what do I need from here to reach the GPA I want". The first is a weighted average nobody should have to do by hand across a dozen courses with different credits; the second is a small piece of algebra most students get wrong by averaging GPAs instead of grade points. This tool does both exactly: the user lists courses (letter grade + credits), picks the 4.0 or 4.3 scale, and gets the weighted GPA computed in exact arithmetic and displayed under one stated rounding rule; a separate solver takes completed credits and current GPA, planned credits, and a target GPA, and answers with the average grade points the planned credits must earn — or says plainly that the target is unreachable on the chosen scale, or already guaranteed. Success: the two answers agree with hand-worked fixtures to the last displayed digit, and the unreachable case is a clear sentence, not a number above the scale maximum presented as if it were a plan. No account, no transcript upload, nothing stored.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Pinned in the SPEC: up to 50 course rows, each an optional text label, a letter grade chosen from the active scale table, credits in 0.5 steps from 0.5 to 30, and an exclude toggle (pass/fail or withdrawn courses the user lists but must not count). Two scale tables ship as auditable data: the 4.0 scale (A 4.0, A− 3.7, B+ 3.3, B 3.0, B− 2.7, C+ 2.3, C 2.0, C− 1.7, D+ 1.3, D 1.0, D− 0.7, F 0.0) and the 4.3 scale (the same plus A+ 4.3). GPA = Σ(points × credits) ÷ Σcredits over included rows, computed exactly (points are tenths and credits are halves, so the core works in integers) and displayed rounded half-up to two decimals — the rounding mode is normative because 3.325 must display 3.33, not the 3.32 that truncation or banker's rounding would give. Solver: completed credits C0 (0–1,000, 0.5 steps) with current GPA G0, planned credits C1 (0.5–300), target T; required average = (T×(C0+C1) − G0×C0) ÷ C1, displayed half-up to two decimals, with three outcomes: feasible (required ≤ scale maximum), unreachable (required > scale maximum — stated as unreachable, with the maximum attainable final GPA shown), and guaranteed (required ≤ 0 — target already secured). Excluded: percentage-score entry and percentage-to-letter conversion, honours/class-rank prediction, institutional policies (retake replacement, forgiveness) — the page says institutions differ and this is the plain weighted rule.

Existing tools: Grade Target （成績目標試算） computes the single grade needed in one course's remaining assessment to reach a desired course score — one course, score domain. This tool aggregates many finished courses into a GPA by credits and solves over a whole future study plan — a different aggregation level, different inputs, different outputs. No other catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task computes GPA; fresh registry/path/draft check remains mandatory before admission. All fixtures — A(3)+B+(4)+C(2) on 4.0 → 29.2/9 = 3.24; A+(3)+A−(3) on 4.3 → 4.00; A(3)+F(3) → 2.00; the 3.325 half-up case A+(1)+B(3) on 4.3 → 3.33; solver 60 credits at 3.00 plus 30 planned for target 3.20 → required 3.60; solver 90 at 2.50 plus 15 planned for target 3.00 → required 6.00, unreachable — are re-derived by script during drafting.
