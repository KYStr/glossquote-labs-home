# Compound Interest and Savings Goal Calculator — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. Money tools earn trust in cents: this specification pins one simulation — monthly compounding on integer cents with monthly rounding — and declares that simulation, not any closed-form formula, to be the product's truth wherever the two disagree.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: individual savers planning an emergency fund, a travel or purchase goal, a child's education pot, or a top-up retirement habit; teachers demonstrating compound growth; and anyone checking whether a monthly amount they can actually afford reaches a target in time. The recurring problem: most calculators silently use closed-form formulas with unstated contribution timing and unrounded intermediates, so two calculators disagree by small amounts and neither can show its working month by month. Success is observable: the user sees the balance after every month under the stated model, the split between their own contributions and earned interest per year, and goal answers (months needed, or the exact minimum monthly contribution to the cent) that are derived from — and verifiable against — that same month-by-month table. Non-goals: investment advice or product recommendation, inflation-adjusted ("real") returns, tax, irregular deposits or withdrawals, multiple accounts/currencies, and debt payoff (the mortgage tool's territory). The page MUST state the model in one plain sentence: interest is compounded monthly at the nominal annual rate ÷ 12, contributions land at month end, and results are a projection of that model, not a promise of any product's return.

Assumptions: a modern desktop browser; a currency label (NT$, US$ or none) that affects display only; no network at runtime.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) Growth mode: initial amount, nominal annual rate %, monthly contribution, term in months (or years + months, stored as months); outputs final balance, total contributions (initial shown separately from recurring), total interest, effective annual rate, a yearly summary table, and an expandable full monthly table (month, interest that month, contribution, end balance); (b) Goal mode A — months to target: initial, rate, monthly contribution, target; outputs the first month whose end balance ≥ target, with that balance, or the honest "not reached within 600 months" result; (c) Goal mode B — required contribution: initial, rate, target, term; outputs the smallest monthly contribution in whole cents whose simulation reaches the target by the term's end, found by the section 4 binary search, and displays the final balance at that contribution and at one cent less (falling short) so the minimality is checkable on the page; (d) a currency-label selector (display only) and CSV export of the monthly table. Bounds: every amount 0 to 999,999,999.99 (initial and target may be 0 where meaningful — see errors), rate 0 to 100 with up to 3 decimals, term 1–600 months, binary-search and simulation loops bounded by those caps. Excluded: beginning-of-month contribution timing (a stated non-option in this version), rate changes over time, fees, and persistence.
Distinction from existing work: the Mortgage Amortization Calculator specification amortises a debt to zero (annuity and equal-principal repayment schedules, prepayment events); this tool grows savings and inverts the question (months-to-target, required contribution) — opposite direction, different outputs, no shared schedule semantics beyond integer-cents discipline. Bill Splitter and Unit Price are one-shot arithmetic with no time dimension. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time simulates compound growth or savings goals; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: Growth mode with initial 10,000.00, rate 12%, contribution 100.00, term 12 months prefilled — the section 4 fixture — so the first screen already shows a verifiable result (final balance 12,536.49, interest 1,336.49). Mode tabs (Growth / Months to target / Required contribution) keep each mode's inputs. Every edit recomputes; tables rebuild from the simulation, never from formulas.
Errors are per-field and specific: negative or over-cap amounts, rate over 100 or with 4+ decimals, term 0 or over 600. Mode-specific meaning rules: in Goal A, a target ≤ initial is answered immediately ("already reached at month 0") rather than erroring; a monthly contribution of 0 with rate 0 and target > initial yields "not reached" with the reason (nothing grows), distinct from the 600-month outcome in wording. In Goal B, a target ≤ initial means the required contribution is 0.00 and the page says so. While any field in the active mode is invalid, outputs freeze marked "out of date". The monthly table is collapsed beyond year 1 by default (a 600-row table is available on demand, not dumped). CSV export serialises the currently computed monthly table exactly. Nothing persists across reload; amounts never appear in the URL or title.

## 4. Computation rules (normative)
Representation: all money is integer cents (amount inputs are parsed to cents exactly; more than 2 decimals in an amount is a parse error). The rate is stored as an exact decimal with up to 3 places; the monthly rate is the rational annualRate/1200, and monthly interest is computed as round-half-away-from-zero(balanceCents × annualRate / 1200) using exact decimal/integer arithmetic — floating-point MUST NOT decide a cent.
Simulation (the contract): balance starts at initialCents. For each month m = 1…term: interest_m = roundHalfAway(balance × annualRate / 1200) cents; balance += interest_m; then balance += contributionCents (end-of-month contribution). Totals: totalInterest = Σ interest_m; totalContributions = term × contributionCents (recurring only; the initial amount is reported separately); finalBalance = initial + totalContributions + totalInterest — this identity MUST hold exactly on the page, which it does by construction. Effective annual rate (display only) = (1 + annualRate/1200)^12 − 1, shown as a percentage to 2 decimals.
Goal A: run the simulation month by month; the answer is the first m with end balance ≥ target, m = 0 when initial ≥ target; stop at 600 with the not-reached result (and, when rate = 0 and contribution = 0 and initial < target, the reason "no growth: rate and contribution are both zero").
Goal B: the required contribution is min { c ∈ cents, c ≥ 0 : simulate(initial, rate, c, term) ≥ target }, found by binary search over c in [0, targetCents] (the target itself is a safe upper bound: contributing the whole target in month 1's place over the term trivially reaches it when initial ≥ 0 — formally, simulation with c = targetCents ends ≥ targetCents because interest is never negative at rate ≥ 0), at most 41 iterations for the capped range; if target ≤ initial the answer is 0 without searching. The page displays simulate(c) and simulate(c − 1) final balances when c > 0.
Worked fixtures (normative, produced by the pinned simulation during drafting): (1) Growth: initial 10,000.00, rate 12%, contribution 100.00, 12 months → monthly rate 1%; final balance **12,536.49**, total interest **1,336.49**, recurring contributions 1,200.00. Note, and the SPEC insists the product's help may say the same: the closed-form formula gives 12,536.50 — one cent more — because it never rounds monthly interest; the simulation governs. Effective annual rate displays **12.68%**. (2) Zero rate: initial 0, rate 0%, contribution 250.00, 12 months → final **3,000.00**, interest 0.00 (pure addition, exactly). (3) Goal A: initial 0, rate 12%, contribution 100.00, target 1,000.00 → reached at month **10** with balance **1,046.22**. (4) Goal B: target 12,000.00, initial 0, term 12, rate 0% → required contribution exactly **1,000.00** (final 12,000.00; at 999.99 final 11,999.88). (5) Goal B: same but rate 12% → required contribution **946.19**, final balance **12,000.05**; at 946.18 the final balance is **11,999.92**, short — the minimality pair the page must show.

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (parser to cents, simulator returning the full monthly series, goal solvers, CSV serialiser) with zero DOM access; a controller; a thin DOM layer. Runtime dependencies: none. Interest arithmetic uses exact integer/decimal maths: balanceCents × rateThousandths fits comfortably in BigInt; the implementation MUST use BigInt (or exact decimal) for the monthly interest product so the cent is never decided by a double at the amount caps (999,999,999.99 × 100% gives a product near 10^14 × 10^3 thousandths — beyond float53 comfort when combined with the division rounding decision).
Privacy/security: amounts are the user's own planning numbers; no network, no storage, no analytics; inputs render as text; CSV is generated locally; no eval/Function; no external resources.
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI, including the model sentence and the formula-vs-simulation note; tables are real tables with headers; mode tabs and fields are native and labelled; the minimality pair in Goal B is ordinary text; keyboard-complete.
Performance bounds: simulation ≤ 600 iterations; Goal B ≤ 41 simulations of ≤ 600 months (≤ 24,600 month-steps) — instant; rendering caps the monthly table behind an expander as stated. Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs offline from file:// or any static host.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages; all three modes; yearly and monthly tables match the simulation; the identity final = initial + contributions + interest holds exactly on every displayed result; CSV equals the monthly table.
- boundaries: term 1 and 600 and 601 refused; rate 0 and 100 and 100.001 refused; amount at cap and one cent over refused; amount with 3 decimals refused; Goal A target ≤ initial (month 0) and no-growth wording; Goal B target ≤ initial (0.00).
- privacy: zero network after load; no storage; amounts absent from URL/title.
- security: field text with markup renders inert; no external resources; no eval/Function.
- usability: a saver answers "how much per month for 12,000 in a year" without instructions; the simulation-vs-formula note is understood as which number governs (spot-check).
- accessibility: real tables with headers; labelled native controls and tabs; keyboard-complete; out-of-date state in text.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent (including the model sentence); correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links; number formatting consistent per locale with the cent values unchanged.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output on Node 24; model constants (1200 divisor, caps, 600) appear once as auditable data matching section 4.
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop; identical cent results for the fixture corpus in every browser (exact arithmetic, no float-decided cents), including at the amount cap.

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests with independently derived expectations: (a) all five section 4 fixtures exactly — balances, interest totals, months, required contributions and both minimality balances; (b) the identity check across a parameter sweep (random-but-seeded cases): final = initial + term × contribution + Σ interest, and the yearly table's last row equals the growth outputs; (c) rounding: a constructed month where balance × rate/1200 lands exactly on a half cent rounds away from zero; (d) Goal B minimality property on a sweep: simulate(c) ≥ target and simulate(c − 1) < target for c > 0; (e) Goal A: first-crossing property (balance at m − 1 < target ≤ balance at m) and the 600-month and no-growth outcomes distinguished; (f) parser: amount decimals > 2 refused, rate 3 decimals accepted and 4 refused, caps enforced.
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): fixtures entered through the real UI in both languages and all three modes; CSV downloaded and diffed against the on-screen monthly table; the Goal B minimality pair visible without interaction beyond mode selection. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
Explicitly NOT_RUN at specification stage: all product tests above; this document claims document completeness only.
