# Time Zone Converter — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. Civil time conversion has exactly two pathological cases and one provenance trap, and this specification is built around all three: a local time in a spring-forward gap does not exist; a local time in a fall-back overlap exists twice; and every offset comes from a time-zone database whose version lives on the user's device, not in this tool. The tool therefore resolves the source reading to one UTC instant under stated gap/overlap rules, derives every target from that single instant, always displays the resolved UTC instant, flags both pathological cases in words, and states the database provenance on the page.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: people coordinating across countries — remote workers arranging calls, travellers sanity-checking itineraries, support teams publishing maintenance windows, families planning video chats. The recurring problem: adding or subtracting a memorised hour difference fails twice a year per hemisphere, fails permanently for half- and quarter-hour zones, and fails silently for the gap and overlap hours, where a naive converter prints a plausible-looking answer for a time that never happens. Success is observable: the user enters one date-time and source zone, picks target zones, and reads a table whose every row is derived from one displayed UTC instant — with day shifts visible, and with explicit flags when the source reading fell in a gap or an overlap. Non-goals: finding a mutually convenient time (that is a search problem, covered elsewhere), recurring conversions, or storing favourite zone sets.

Assumptions: a modern desktop or mobile browser whose Intl implementation carries a reasonably current time-zone database; dates within 2000–2099; no network at runtime; nothing is stored.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) a source date (2000-01-01–2099-12-31) and time (HH:MM, minute precision, 24-hour) with a source zone chosen from the shipped list of section 4; (b) 1–20 target zones from the same list, chosen by the user, duplicates refused (the same zone twice in the target list is a visible note, not a second row); the source zone may be included as a target, in which case its row shows the *resolved* source reading (which, in a gap case, differs from what the user typed — that difference is the point of the flag); (c) resolution of the source reading to a UTC instant under section 4's gap/overlap rules, with the instant displayed in UTC next to the inputs; (d) one row per target: IANA zone name, converted local date and time, UTC offset at that instant formatted ±HH:MM, and day shift relative to the source calendar date (−1, 0 or +1, in words at least in the row's accessible text); (e) the provenance statement of section 5. Excluded: window/slot search; recurring or multi-date conversion; seconds precision; zone selection by map or city search beyond the shipped list; historical dates before 2000; saving sets.
Distinction from existing work: Meeting Overlap searches one day for a continuous window when several people's working hours intersect — its input is windows and its output is a slot; it never converts a single instant, shows no offsets, and has no gap/overlap semantics. This tool's input is one instant-as-read-in-a-zone and its output is that instant read in other zones, with the pathological cases specified. Date Calculator adds/subtracts calendar durations; Frame Timecode converts media timestamps; neither touches civil time zones. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time performs IANA conversion of a chosen date-time; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: source prefilled with the worked example — 2026-01-15 09:00, Asia/Taipei — and targets prefilled with America/New_York, Europe/London, Australia/Sydney and Asia/Kathmandu, so fixture Z1's table and the UTC instant line are visible on first load. Editing the date, time or source zone, or adding/removing targets, recomputes the whole table from the newly resolved instant. When the source reading is in a gap, a banner above the table states that the entered local time does not occur in the source zone on that date (clocks jump over it), that it has been shifted forward by the size of the jump, and shows the resolved source reading; when it is in an overlap, the banner states the local time occurs twice and that the earlier occurrence is being used. No banner appears for ordinary readings. An invalid date (e.g. 2026-02-30) or a date outside 2000–2099 is a field error: the table freezes at its last valid state marked out of date. Adding a 21st target is refused with a note; adding a duplicate target is refused with a note naming the zone. Removing all targets leaves an empty-table prompt (the resolved instant still displays). Nothing persists across reload; the entered date-time never appears in the URL or title.

## 4. Computation rules (normative)
Zone list (normative data; MUST ship once as data, exactly these 40 IANA names, in this order): Pacific/Auckland, Australia/Sydney, Australia/Melbourne, Australia/Perth, Asia/Ulaanbaatar, Asia/Vladivostok, Asia/Tokyo, Asia/Seoul, Asia/Shanghai, Asia/Taipei, Asia/Hong_Kong, Asia/Singapore, Asia/Manila, Asia/Jakarta, Asia/Kathmandu, Asia/Kolkata, Asia/Dhaka, Asia/Karachi, Asia/Tehran, Asia/Dubai, Africa/Cairo, Africa/Johannesburg, Europe/Istanbul, Europe/Moscow, Europe/Athens, Europe/Paris, Europe/London, Europe/Lisbon, Atlantic/Reykjavik, America/Sao_Paulo, America/Bogota, America/Toronto, America/Mexico_City, America/New_York, America/Chicago, America/Denver, America/Los_Angeles, America/Anchorage, Pacific/Honolulu, America/Santiago. A build check MUST verify the count is 40 and that the platform Intl implementation accepts every name; a rejected name is a build failure, not a runtime surprise.
Resolution (normative): all conversion is delegated to the platform's time-zone database through Intl. The core resolves the source reading (date, time, source zone) to a UTC instant as follows. Let the reading be interpreted first under the offset in effect immediately before any transition at that reading; equivalently: (gap) if the local reading does not occur, the resolved instant is the reading shifted forward by the transition's jump size and read under the post-transition offset — the instant whose post-transition local reading is (entered reading + jump); (overlap) if the local reading occurs twice, the resolved instant is the EARLIER of the two occurrences (the one with the pre-transition, typically daylight, offset); (ordinary) the unique instant. Whether a reading is in a gap or an overlap MUST be determined by round-tripping: formatting the resolved instant back into the source zone and comparing with the entered reading (a mismatch of exactly the jump size indicates a gap; the existence of a second instant formatting to the same reading indicates an overlap) — the implementation MUST NOT hardcode transition dates.
Derivation: every target row is produced by formatting the single resolved instant in that zone via Intl; offsets shown are the zone's UTC offset at that instant, formatted sign + two-digit hours + ':' + two-digit minutes (e.g. +05:45, −05:00, +00:00 for UTC-zero — never "Z" and never a bare name like "EST" in place of the numeric offset; an abbreviation MAY accompany, never replace, the number). Day shift compares the target row's calendar date with the *entered source date* (not the resolved reading's date): −1, 0 or +1 (within the supported range and zone list, larger shifts cannot occur and MUST NOT be fabricated).
Fixtures (derived against the reference tz database current at drafting; implementations MUST reproduce them on a current database, and a future database change that alters a fixture is handled by the section 5 provenance rule, not by editing results silently):
- Z1: source 2026-01-15 09:00 Asia/Taipei → instant 2026-01-15 01:00 UTC. Rows: America/New_York 2026-01-14 20:00, −05:00, day −1; Europe/London 2026-01-15 01:00, +00:00, day 0; Australia/Sydney 2026-01-15 12:00, +11:00, day 0; Asia/Kathmandu 2026-01-15 06:45, +05:45, day 0.
- Z2: source 2026-07-15 09:00 Asia/Taipei → instant 01:00 UTC; America/New_York row: 2026-07-14 21:00, −04:00, day −1 (DST in effect — the offset differs from Z1, which is the point of the fixture).
- Z3 (gap): source 2026-03-08 02:30 America/New_York does not occur (clocks jump 02:00 → 03:00). Resolved instant: 2026-03-08 07:30 UTC; the resolved source reading is 03:30 (−04:00); the gap banner shows; a target row for Asia/Taipei reads 2026-03-08 15:30 (+08:00), day 0.
- Z4 (overlap): source 2026-11-01 01:30 America/New_York occurs twice; the earlier occurrence (−04:00) is used → instant 2026-11-01 05:30 UTC; the overlap banner shows; Asia/Taipei row: 2026-11-01 13:30 (+08:00), day 0.
- Z5 (source as target in a gap): Z3 with America/New_York also in the targets → its row shows 03:30, −04:00 (the resolved reading), not the typed 02:30.

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (resolution with round-trip gap/overlap detection, instant formatting, offset and day-shift derivation) with zero DOM access, over the platform Intl; a controller; a thin DOM layer. The 40-zone list ships once as auditable data matching section 4. Runtime dependencies: none — in particular no bundled time-zone database: the platform database is the single source, which is precisely why the provenance statement exists.
Privacy/security: no network, no storage, no analytics; there is no free text anywhere (dates, times and zones come from native controls and the shipped list); no eval/Function; no external resources.
Honesty rules (normative, testable): (1) Provenance statement, always visible with the table, with this content: conversions use the time-zone database built into your browser/device; if a government changes a zone's rules, devices with an older database can show different results until updated. (2) The resolved UTC instant is always displayed with the results. (3) Gap and overlap banners use the section 3 wording substance — an implementation that silently normalises either case is defective even if its numbers match the rules. (4) No row, heading or meta text claims the tool accounts for future rule changes.
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI, including banners, the provenance statement and day-shift wording. Native labelled date/time inputs and zone selects; the table is a real table with headers; day shift is text ("previous day" / "same day" / "next day" plus the −1/0/+1 marker), not colour alone; banners are text blocks, keyboard-reachable in reading order. Performance bounds: at most 21 Intl resolutions per edit. Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs offline from file:// or any static host.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages; fixtures Z1–Z5 reproduced exactly, including offsets, day shifts, the resolved instants and both banners.
- boundaries: dates 2000-01-01 and 2099-12-31 accepted, 1999-12-31 and an invalid calendar date refused with the freeze behaviour; 20 targets accepted, 21st refused; duplicate target refused by name; empty target list shows the prompt with the instant still displayed.
- privacy: zero network after load; no storage; entered date-time absent from URL/title and never transmitted.
- security: no external resources; no eval/Function; no injectable free text exists.
- usability: a user converts a stated meeting time to three zones and reads off the day shift for the one that changes date, without instructions (spot-check).
- accessibility: labelled native controls; real table with headers; day shift and banners conveyed in text, not colour alone; keyboard-complete.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent (including banners and the provenance statement); correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output on Node 24; the zone list appears once as auditable data, build-verified to count 40 and to be Intl-accepted name by name; the provenance statement is present in both locales' rendered pages (DOM check); Node 24.
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop and mobile-width layout; fixtures Z1–Z4 give identical results in each browser's current Intl database (a discrepancy traceable to a documented database-version difference is reported as such, not silently passed).

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests with independently derived expectations: (a) fixtures Z1–Z5 exactly, every field of every row named; (b) gap/overlap detection by round-trip on synthetic transitions from at least two southern-hemisphere zones (transitions in the opposite season — e.g. Australia/Sydney 2026-04-05 overlap and 2026-10-04 gap, verified against the platform database at test time, not hardcoded expectations of the implementation's own making); (c) the single-instant property: for random valid inputs, every target row re-resolved to an instant equals the displayed UTC instant exactly; (d) day-shift derivation across the date line in both directions (Auckland vs Honolulu on the same instant); (e) bounds: date limits, the 20-target cap, duplicate refusal; (f) offset formatting: +05:45, −05:00, +00:00 cases.
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): entering Z3 shows the gap banner and the 03:30 resolved reading; entering Z4 shows the overlap banner; the provenance statement is visible with the table on a mobile-width viewport. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
Explicitly NOT_RUN at specification stage: all product tests above; this document claims document completeness only.
