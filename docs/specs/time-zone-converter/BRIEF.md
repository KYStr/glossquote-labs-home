# Time Zone Converter — preparatory BRIEF v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. Full normative contract is SPEC.md.

## 1. Goal
Anyone arranging a call, a flight connection or a release across countries asks the same simple question: "9:00 Tuesday here is what, exactly, over there" — and the dangerous answers are the confident wrong ones produced around daylight-saving transitions, when a local time can occur twice or not at all. This tool converts one chosen date-time in one IANA time zone into a user-picked list of other IANA zones (up to 20 targets from a shipped list of 40), showing for each the converted date-time, its UTC offset, and the day shift relative to the source date. Its defining honesty: daylight-saving behaviour is delegated to the device's own time-zone database via the platform Intl implementation, the resolved UTC instant is always displayed so any result can be checked, and the two pathological cases are named on the page — a source time inside a spring-forward gap is shifted forward by the gap and flagged, and a source time inside a fall-back overlap uses the earlier occurrence and is flagged. Success: the fixtures, including a gap case, an ambiguous case and a 45-minute-offset zone, reproduce exactly on the reference implementation, and no conversion is ever presented without its flags. No account, no meeting scheduling, nothing stored.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Pinned in the SPEC: source = one date (2000-01-01 to 2099-12-31) and time (minute precision) in one zone chosen from the shipped 40-zone list; targets = 1–20 zones from the same list (the source may also appear as a target, showing the resolved source reading). Conversion goes through a single resolved UTC instant: the source reading is resolved under the gap/overlap rules, then every target reading is derived from that instant by the platform tz database — targets are never converted pairwise from each other. Every row shows: zone name, converted local date-time, UTC offset (±HH:MM), and day shift (−1/0/+1 relative to the source calendar date). The page states that offsets come from the device's time-zone database and can differ across devices if governments change rules after that database was published. Excluded: searching for mutually convenient meeting windows, recurring events, flight durations, historical dates before 2000, and zone-map/location pickers.

Existing tools: Meeting Overlap takes several people's working windows and searches for a shared slot on one day — a scheduling search, with no single-instant conversion table, no offsets and no gap/overlap handling. Frame Timecode and Date Calculator do media/arithmetic time, not civil time zones. No other catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task converts a date-time across IANA zones; fresh registry/path/draft check remains mandatory before admission. All fixtures — 2026-01-15 09:00 Asia/Taipei → New York 2026-01-14 20:00 (−05:00, day −1), London 01:00 (+00:00), Sydney 12:00 (+11:00), Kathmandu 06:45 (+05:45); the same in July → New York 2026-07-14 21:00 EDT; gap case 2026-03-08 02:30 America/New_York resolved forward to the instant shown in New York as 03:30 EDT (07:30 UTC) with the gap flag; ambiguous case 2026-11-01 01:30 America/New_York taken at its earlier (EDT) occurrence → 05:30 UTC → Taipei 13:30, with the overlap flag — are re-derived by script during drafting.
