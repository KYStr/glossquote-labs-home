# Electricity Cost Calculator — preparatory BRIEF v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. Full normative contract is SPEC.md.

## 1. Goal
Everyone with an electricity bill eventually asks the same question — "what is this appliance actually costing me?" — and meets calculators that assume a rate they cannot change, bury the arithmetic, or quote a national tariff table that is out of date the day it ships. Success: the user enters an appliance's watts, hours per day, days in the period and quantity, types the rate from their own bill (single flat rate, or a simple volume-tiered tariff of up to three bands), picks a currency, and gets energy per day and per period in kWh plus cost per day and per period, with the tier breakdown shown when tiers apply — every number reproducible from the formulas printed on the page. No built-in tariff table that can go stale, no account, no upload: the inputs never leave the page.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Pinned in the SPEC: energy = watts ÷ 1000 × hours/day × days × quantity, computed unrounded; kWh displayed to 3 decimals; money computed from unrounded kWh and rounded only at display, half away from zero, into integer minor units (cents for TWD/USD/EUR/GBP, whole yen for JPY) — the integer-minor-unit discipline means a displayed total can never drift from its tier subtotals by more than the stated rounding rule, and the tier table rounds each subtotal for display while the total is the rounded exact sum, with any 1-minor-unit display difference explicitly the rule, not a bug. Inputs: watts 1–50,000 (decimals allowed), hours/day more than 0 and at most 24 (decimals allowed), period days 1–366 whole, quantity 1–100 whole, an optional free-text appliance label used only in the heading. Tariff modes: flat (one rate per kWh, more than 0 and at most 999.999) or tiered (two or three bands: ascending whole-kWh thresholds, a rate per band, the last band open-ended). A mandatory honesty note for tiered mode states the calculation treats the entered usage as the entire billed consumption for the period — the usual simplification — because one appliance's true slice of a real tiered bill depends on the household total. Excluded: built-in utility rate tables, time-of-use pricing by hour of day, demand charges, taxes and fixed fees (the user can fold them into their rate themselves — stated), multiple different appliances in one list, standby-power databases, solar/export offsets, and persistence.

Existing tools: no catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task prices electricity usage. Subscription Budget tracks recurring money amounts; Unit Price compares shop prices per unit; Compound Interest grows money over time — none converts watts and hours into kWh and tiered cost. Fresh registry/path/draft check remains mandatory before admission. All fixtures — flat-rate heater and air-conditioner cases, a four-bulb quantity case, a three-tier 350 kWh case with exact band subtotals, a whole-yen JPY case, and a rounding case (0.999 kWh at a repeating-decimal rate) — are re-derived by script during drafting.
