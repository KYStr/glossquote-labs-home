# Electricity Cost Calculator — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. "What does this appliance cost me?" is simple multiplication that tools complicate by assuming the rate, shipping a tariff table that ages, or rounding intermediate kWh before pricing it. This specification keeps the user's own rate, prices the unrounded energy, rounds money once at display into integer minor units, and states plainly what a tiered calculation can and cannot claim about one appliance inside a household bill.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: households checking a heater, air-conditioner or dehumidifier against a real bill, renters splitting utilities, small shops and stalls pricing a freezer's running cost, and anyone deciding whether an appliance upgrade pays. The recurring problem: calculators that hard-code a country's rates (wrong for the user's plan and wrong next year), round the kWh before multiplying, or present one appliance's "tiered cost" as if the household's other usage did not exist. Success is observable: the user enters watts, hours per day, days in the period and quantity, supplies the rate from their own bill — flat, or up to three volume tiers — picks a currency, and receives kWh per day and per period, cost per day and per period, and, in tiered mode, a breakdown table by band. The formulas are printed on the page; the tiered-mode limitation is printed beside the result. Non-goals: being a tariff database, modelling a whole household, or predicting a bill — the page MUST NOT claim the period cost is the user's bill, only the entered usage priced at the entered rates.

Assumptions: a modern desktop browser; the user knows their appliance watts (nameplate or manual — the page says where to find it) and their rate (from their bill); no network at runtime.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) one appliance per calculation with an optional free-text label shown in the result heading; (b) inputs — power in watts, hours per day, period length in whole days, quantity of identical units; (c) tariff modes — flat rate, or tiered with two or three bands (each band: an upper threshold in whole kWh and a rate; the final band open-ended); (d) currency selector: TWD, USD, EUR, GBP (two minor decimals, symbol prefix NT$, US$, €, £) and JPY (zero minor decimals, symbol ¥); (e) outputs — kWh per day, kWh per period, cost per day, cost per period, and the tier breakdown table in tiered mode; (f) the formulas printed on the page. Accepted ranges: watts more than 0 and at most 50,000 (decimals allowed, at most one decimal place); hours per day more than 0 and at most 24 (at most two decimal places); period days whole, 1–366; quantity whole, 1–100; any rate more than 0 and at most 999.999 (at most three decimal places); tier thresholds whole kWh, strictly ascending, each 1–1,000,000. Excluded: built-in rates for any utility (including Taipower), time-of-use (hour-of-day) pricing, demand (kW) charges, taxes, fixed monthly fees, fuel adjustments, multi-appliance lists, appliance wattage databases, solar self-consumption or export, and persistence.
Distinction from existing work: Subscription Budget sums recurring subscription prices; Unit Price compares per-unit shopping prices; Compound Interest models money growth; Mortgage Amortization schedules loan payments. None converts power × time into energy and prices it against flat or tiered per-kWh rates. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time prices electricity usage; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: flat mode, TWD, a worked example prefilled (electric heater, 1000 W, 3 h/day, 30 days, quantity 1, rate 5.000) with results shown — 3.000 kWh/day, 90.000 kWh, NT$15.00/day, NT$450.00 — so the arithmetic can be checked before the user types anything. Switching to tiered mode reveals the band editor prefilled with two bands (up to 120 kWh at the previous flat rate, above that a second rate field) and the limitation note. Every edit recomputes together; switching tariff mode or currency never clears the usage inputs.
Error states are per-field and specific: zero or negative watts/hours/rate; hours above 24; non-whole days, quantity or tier thresholds; too many decimal places (the allowed precision is named); tier thresholds not strictly ascending (the offending band is named); quantity or days at zero. While any error stands, outputs freeze at the last valid state marked "out of date". The optional label is display-only: it appears in the heading and nowhere else, and is never sent or stored. Nothing persists across reload; inputs never appear in the URL or title.

## 4. Computation rules (normative)
Energy: kWh_per_day = (watts ÷ 1000) × hours_per_day × quantity; kWh_period = kWh_per_day × period_days. Both are kept unrounded internally; displayed rounded to three decimals, half away from zero.
Money discipline: every cost is computed from the unrounded energy and rounded once, at display, half away from zero, to the currency's minor unit (2 decimals; JPY 0). Cost_per_day uses kWh_per_day; cost_per_period uses kWh_period. Because displays round independently, the pair (displayed per-day cost × days) may differ from the displayed period cost by a minor unit or two — the period figure is normative, and the page's formula note says costs are computed from unrounded energy and rounded only for display.
Flat mode: cost = kWh × rate for the respective span.
Tiered mode: the period energy kWh_period is priced band by band: band i covers the energy above band (i−1)'s threshold up to its own threshold at its rate; the final band is open-ended. The breakdown table shows, per band actually used: band range (e.g. "0–120 kWh", "120–300 kWh", "above 300 kWh"), kWh in band (3 decimals), rate, and subtotal (rounded for display from that band's unrounded subtotal). The period cost is the rounded value of the exact sum of unrounded subtotals — NOT the sum of the displayed subtotals; if the two differ by one minor unit, the table's total row still shows the exact-sum value, and the formula note's rounding sentence covers this. Per-day cost in tiered mode = period cost (exact) ÷ period_days, rounded at display, labelled an average per day (tiered pricing has no true per-day cost; the label MUST say "average").
Tiered limitation (normative wording, both languages, beside the result): "This treats the usage above as your whole billed consumption for the period. On a real tiered bill, one appliance's share of each band depends on your household's total usage, so treat this as an estimate for the usage entered, not a slice of an actual bill."
Number display: thousands are grouped with commas and the decimal separator is a point in both languages (calculator convention, stated in the formula note); the currency symbol prefixes the amount with no space (NT$450.00, ¥2,790).
Worked fixtures (normative, re-derived by script during drafting):
- F1 (initial example): 1000 W, 3 h/day, 30 days, qty 1, flat TWD 5.000 → 3.000 kWh/day, 90.000 kWh; cost NT$15.00/day, **NT$450.00** period.
- F2: air conditioner 1200 W, 8 h/day, 30 days, flat 4.500 → 9.600 kWh/day, 288.000 kWh; period **NT$1,296.00**.
- F3: four bulbs, 60 W each entered as watts 60 with quantity 4, 5 h/day, 30 days, flat USD 0.300 → 1.200 kWh/day, 36.000 kWh; period **US$10.80**.
- F4 (tiered): 2000 W, 7 h/day, 25 days → 350.000 kWh; bands up to 120 @ 3.000, up to 300 @ 4.000, above @ 5.000 → band kWh 120 / 180 / 50, subtotals 360.00 / 720.00 / 250.00, period **NT$1,330.00**, average per day NT$53.20.
- F5 (JPY): the F1 usage at flat JPY 31 → period **¥2,790** (no decimals anywhere in JPY outputs).
- F6 (rounding): 333 W, 1 h/day, 3 days → 0.999 kWh exactly; flat rate 3.333 → exact cost 3.329667 → displayed **NT$3.33**; displayed per-day cost NT$1.11 (exact 1.109889), and 1.11 × 3 = 3.33 here agrees, while the normative rule remains the unrounded computation.

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (validation, energy, flat and tiered pricing, currency formatting) with zero DOM access; a controller; a thin DOM layer. All constants (input ranges, decimal-place limits, currency minor units and symbols, band count limits) appear once as auditable data matching section 4. Money MUST be handled so that display rounding is the only rounding: implementations should compute in a way that reproduces the fixtures exactly (integer minor-unit arithmetic after the single final rounding, or equivalent care with doubles — the fixtures, including F6's 3.329667 → 3.33, are the test).
Privacy/security: no network, no storage, no analytics; the appliance label and all numbers render as text only; no eval/Function; no external resources.
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI, including the tiered-limitation wording and the rounding sentence. Breakdown and results are real tables/text; all inputs native and labelled; the band editor's add/remove-band controls are buttons with text labels; errors associated with their fields; keyboard-complete flow.
Performance bounds: trivial arithmetic; band count ≤ 3. Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs offline from file:// or any static host.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages; flat and tiered modes (2 and 3 bands); all five currencies; every section 4 fixture reproduced exactly, including the tier breakdown rows of F4 and the average-per-day label in tiered mode.
- boundaries: watts just above 0 and 50,000; hours 24 accepted and 24.01 refused; days 1 and 366; quantity 1 and 100; decimal-place limits per field; tier thresholds equal or descending refused with the band named; usage landing exactly on a tier threshold (all energy in the lower bands, none above).
- privacy: zero network after load; no storage; label and numbers absent from URL/title.
- security: the free-text label containing markup renders inert in the heading and everywhere else; no external resources; no eval/Function.
- usability: a renter prices a heater from a nameplate wattage and a bill rate without instructions, and can state from the limitation note what the tiered figure does and does not mean (spot-check).
- accessibility: real tables with headers for results and breakdown; labelled native inputs and band controls; errors associated; keyboard-complete including adding and removing a band.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent (including limitation wording and formula note); correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output on Node 24; constants appear once as auditable data matching section 4; money formatting constants (minor units, symbols) in exactly one place.
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop; identical outputs for the fixture corpus across browsers.

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests with independently derived expectations: (a) fixtures F1–F6 exactly (energies to 3 decimals, money to the minor unit, F4's band rows and average); (b) tier edges: energy exactly at the first threshold; energy one kWh into band 2; a two-band tariff; a band with zero energy omitted from the breakdown; (c) rounding: a constructed cost at exactly x.xx5 rounds half away from zero; the F6 rule that period cost comes from unrounded values even when per-day displays suggest otherwise; JPY zero-decimal formatting with comma grouping; (d) validation: every range and precision refusal in section 4, threshold ordering, and the label carried through as inert text; (e) mode behaviour: switching flat ↔ tiered preserves usage inputs and recomputes; currency switching reformats without changing any underlying value.
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): fixtures entered through the real UI in both languages; a band added and removed through the real controls; stale-state behaviour on an invalid field; the limitation note visible beside tiered results and absent in flat mode. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
Explicitly NOT_RUN at specification stage: all product tests above; this document claims document completeness only.
