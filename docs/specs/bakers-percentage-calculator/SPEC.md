# Baker's Percentage Calculator — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. Baker's percentages have one basis rule that every error in this domain traces back to: percentages are relative to the *combined flour weight* — never to total dough weight, and never flour-relative-to-itself per flour. This specification pins that rule down as an enforced invariant (flour-flagged percentages MUST sum to exactly 100, and the page refuses to silently renormalise), computes in exact decimal arithmetic, and states its single rounding rule, so three modes — from total weight, from flour weight, and reverse from weights — can never drift apart.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: home bakers scaling a bread formula to fit a particular tin or appetite, and small bakery staff converting between a formula card (percentages) and a bench sheet (grams). The recurring problem: a formula published as percentages is useless at the bench until converted, and the conversion breaks in predictable places — two flours whose percentages must share the 100 basis, a target total dough weight that requires dividing by the percentage sum, and hydration quoted from memory instead of computed. Success is observable: the user enters the formula once and gets exact weights for a chosen total or flour weight, or enters weighed amounts and gets the percentages and hydration back, with all three modes agreeing on shared fixtures. Non-goals: teaching baking, advising on formula quality, or handling volume measures — grams and percentages only.

Assumptions: a modern desktop or mobile browser; the user knows which ingredients are flours and which count as water (the flags are theirs to set; the tool never guesses from names); no network at runtime; nothing is stored.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) up to 30 ingredient rows, each: name (text), percentage (0–1,000, at most one decimal place), a flour flag and a water flag (a row may be both only if it is flour — a water-flagged flour row is refused as a flag error, section 3); (b) the basis invariant of section 4 enforced in percentage modes; (c) Mode A — target total dough weight → per-ingredient weights; (d) Mode B — flour weight → per-ingredient weights plus implied total; (e) Mode C — per-ingredient weights → percentages, implied total and hydration; (f) hydration readout in every mode; (g) row add/remove. Excluded: volume units and imperial weights; preferments, levains and multi-stage builds (one flat formula only); baker's math for enriched/yeast conversions beyond plain percentages; saving or sharing formulas; nutrition or cost.
Distinction from existing work: Recipe Scaler takes a finished recipe in amounts and multiplies every amount by a servings factor — representation never changes, there is no flour basis, no percentage concept, and no hydration. This tool exists precisely to convert between the percentage representation and weights, in both directions, under the flour-basis invariant. Unit Converter converts one quantity between two units; it has no multi-row formula semantics. Paint/Fuel calculators divide single totals by rates — generic arithmetic sharing nothing with the flour-basis rule. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time implements baker's percentages; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: Mode A, three rows prefilled as the worked example — Bread flour 100 (flour), Water 65 (water), Salt 2 — target total 840 g, outputs showing fixture K1's weights, so the first screen is already a checked example. Editing any percentage, flag or target recomputes all weights together. In percentage modes the flour-basis indicator is always visible: it shows the current flour-flagged sum; when the sum is not exactly 100 the outputs freeze at their last valid state marked out of date, and the indicator states the current sum and that flour percentages must total 100 (e.g. after adding a second flour at 20 without reducing the first). Mode switching preserves the rows: switching A↔B changes only the basis input; switching to C turns the percentage column into a weight column (starting from the currently displayed weights, rounded values becoming the inputs — the page notes this), and switching back recomputes percentages from those weights. A row flagged both flour and water, a percentage with more than one decimal, a percentage above 1,000, a target outside 1–100,000 g, or (in mode C) a flour-weight sum of zero are field errors with the same freeze-and-mark behaviour. Removing rows is free except that percentage modes require at least one flour row to exist before outputs resume. Nothing persists across reload; no figure appears in the URL or title.

## 4. Computation rules (normative)
Representation: percentages are exact decimals with one decimal place (integer tenths of a percent internally); weights are grams. All core arithmetic is exact rational/decimal; display rounding happens once per displayed figure, round half-up to 0.1 (weights in g, percentages in %, hydration in %).
Basis invariant (percentage modes A and B): let F = Σ p_i over flour-flagged rows. Outputs are valid only when F = 100 exactly. The tool MUST NOT renormalise, rescale or "fix" the flours silently.
Mode A: given target total T (1–100,000 g) and S = Σ p_i over ALL rows: weight_i = T × p_i ÷ S (exact), displayed at 0.1 g. Because each displayed weight is rounded independently, the displayed weights may sum to T ± 0.1×rows/2; the page shows the exact total T (not the sum of rounded weights) as the total — this is stated on the page.
Mode B: given flour weight W: weight_i = W × p_i ÷ 100; implied total = W × S ÷ 100 (displayed 0.1 g).
Mode C: given weights w_i (0–100,000 g each): flour total Wf = Σ w_i over flour-flagged rows (MUST be > 0); p_i = w_i ÷ Wf × 100, displayed at 0.1; implied total = Σ w_i.
Hydration: in modes A/B, hydration = Σ p_i over water-flagged rows (a percentage figure, displayed 0.1 — by the basis rule this equals water weight ÷ flour weight × 100). In mode C, hydration = (Σ w_i over water-flagged rows) ÷ Wf × 100, displayed 0.1. Only flagged rows count; ingredient names are never inspected.
Bounds: rows 1–30 (a 31st refused with a note); percentage 0–1,000.0 per row; a non-flour, non-water row at 0% is legal and weighs 0.0 g.
Fixtures (independently derived; implementations MUST reproduce exactly):
- K1 (Mode A): rows flour 100, water 65, salt 2, yeast 1 (S = 168), T = 840 → 500.0 / 325.0 / 10.0 / 5.0 g; hydration 65.0.
- K2 (Mode B): same formula, W = 600 → 600.0 / 390.0 / 12.0 / 6.0 g; implied total 1,008.0 g.
- K3 (Mode C): weights flour 500, water 325, salt 10 → percentages 100.0 / 65.0 / 2.0; total 835.0 g; hydration 65.0.
- K4 (two flours): bread flour 80 (flour), whole-wheat flour 20 (flour), water 70 (water) (S = 170), Mode A T = 850 → 400.0 / 100.0 / 350.0 g; hydration 70.0.
- K5 (rounding): flour 100, water 63 (S = 163), Mode A T = 500 → flour 306.7 g (306.748…), water 193.3 g (193.251…); displayed sum 500.0.
- K6 (invariant): K4 with the whole-wheat row deleted and nothing else changed leaves flour sum 80 → outputs frozen/marked, indicator shows 80 and the must-total-100 message; no renormalised weights are shown.

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (basis validation, the three mode computations, hydration, formatting) with zero DOM access; a controller; a thin DOM layer. The bounds and rounding rule appear once as auditable constants matching section 4. Runtime dependencies: none.
Privacy/security: no network, no storage, no analytics; ingredient names are the only free text and render as escaped text only; all numbers parse under the strict decimal rule (one decimal place for percentages) — locale commas are not silently accepted inside numeric fields; no eval/Function; no external resources.
Honesty rules (normative, testable): percentages are always labelled as flour-relative; the Mode A total note (exact total vs sum of rounded weights) is printed with the results; hydration is described as "water-flagged ingredients ÷ flour" and the page never infers water content from an ingredient's name (milk, eggs etc. count only if the user flags them); no formula-quality or health claim is made.
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI, including the basis indicator, the invariant message and the total-rounding note. Native labelled inputs and toggles; the basis state is text, not colour alone; results are a real table with headers; keyboard-complete flow. Performance bounds: 30 rows of decimal arithmetic per edit. Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs offline from file:// or any static host.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages and all three modes; fixtures K1–K6 reproduced exactly, including hydration in each mode and the K6 freeze behaviour.
- boundaries: 30 rows accepted, 31st refused; percentage 0 and 1,000.0 accepted, 1,000.1 and two-decimal percentages refused; targets 1 and 100,000 g accepted, 0 refused; mode C with zero flour weight is a field error, not a crash or NaN.
- privacy: zero network after load; no storage; formula and figures absent from URL/title and never transmitted.
- security: an ingredient name containing markup renders inert; no external resources; no eval/Function.
- usability: a baker enters a two-flour formula and gets bench weights for a 1 kg total without instructions, and can state the hydration from the page (spot-check).
- accessibility: labelled native controls and toggles; basis state and errors in text, not colour alone; results as a real table; keyboard-complete.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent (including the invariant message and rounding note); correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output on Node 24; bounds and the rounding rule appear once as auditable constants matching section 4; Node 24.
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop and mobile-width layout; fixtures give identical results across browsers.

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests with independently derived expectations: (a) fixtures K1–K6 exactly, every displayed figure; (b) round-trip property: for generated valid formulas, Mode C applied to Mode A's exact (unrounded) weights returns the original percentages exactly, and Mode B's implied total equals Mode A run at that total; (c) invariant: flour sums of 99.9 and 100.1 both freeze outputs (the comparison is exact, not tolerance-based); (d) flag errors: flour+water row refused; water flag on a non-flour row works (e.g. milk row); (e) bounds: every section 4 limit at and beyond; (f) rounding: a constructed case at a 0.05 g boundary rounds half-up.
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): the K4 two-flour flow entered by hand yields 400/100/350; adding a flour without rebalancing triggers the visible invariant state; switching A→C→A preserves the formula within the stated rounding note. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
Explicitly NOT_RUN at specification stage: all product tests above; this document claims document completeness only.
