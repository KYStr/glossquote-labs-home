# Baker's Percentage Calculator — preparatory BRIEF v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. Full normative contract is SPEC.md.

## 1. Goal
Professional and serious home bakers do not write bread formulas in grams — they write them in baker's percentages, where total flour is always 100% and every other ingredient is a percentage of the flour weight, because that is what makes a formula scalable and comparable. Converting between the two representations by hand is error-prone exactly where it hurts: with several flours, a target dough weight, and rounding at the scale. This tool holds one formula as a list of ingredients (each with a percentage, a flour flag, and a water flag) and answers three questions exactly: given a target total dough weight, what does each ingredient weigh; given a flour weight, the same; and given actual weights, what percentages — and what hydration — do they represent. Success: the worked fixtures agree to the last displayed decimal, the flour basis is never ambiguous (percentages are always relative to the *combined* flour weight, the single most common misunderstanding), and the hydration figure is the water-flagged percentage total, not a guess from ingredient names. No account, no saved formulas, nothing stored.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Pinned in the SPEC: up to 30 ingredient rows (name text, percentage 0–1,000 with at most one decimal, flour flag, water flag). Invariants: at least one flour-flagged row, and flour-flagged percentages MUST sum to exactly 100 — a formula whose flours sum to anything else is an error state with the current sum shown, because silently renormalising would change the baker's formula. Mode A (target total dough weight 1–100,000 g): weight_i = total × p_i ÷ Σp over all rows, computed exactly and displayed rounded half-up to 0.1 g. Mode B (flour weight 1–100,000 g): weight_i = flourWeight × p_i ÷ 100. Mode C (reverse, from weights 0–100,000 g per row): p_i = weight_i ÷ (Σ weights of flour-flagged rows) × 100, displayed half-up to 0.1; a row with weight 0 shows 0.0. Hydration readout = Σ percentages of water-flagged rows (in modes A/B) or the equivalent weight ratio in mode C, displayed to 0.1. Outputs also include total dough weight implied by modes B and C. Excluded: volume units (cups), imperial weights, preferment/levain sub-formulas, temperature or timing advice, and nutrition.

Existing tools: Recipe Scaler multiplies a finished recipe's ingredient amounts by a servings factor — every amount scales together and there is no flour basis, no percentages, no hydration. This tool's whole subject is the percentage representation itself and its exact conversion to and from weights. Unit Converter converts single quantities between units; it knows nothing of formulas. No other catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task implements baker's percentages; fresh registry/path/draft check remains mandatory before admission. All fixtures — flour 100 / water 65 / salt 2 / yeast 1 (Σp 168) at 840 g total → 500 / 325 / 10 / 5 g; the same formula at 600 g flour → water 390, salt 12, yeast 6, total 1,008 g; reverse weights 500 / 325 / 10 → 100.0 / 65.0 / 2.0 and hydration 65.0; two flours 80 + 20 with water 70 at 850 g → 400 / 100 / 350 g; and the rounding case flour 100 / water 63 at 500 g → 306.7 / 193.3 g — are re-derived by script during drafting.
