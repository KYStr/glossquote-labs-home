# Cron Expression Explainer and Next-Run Calculator — preparatory BRIEF v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. Full normative contract is SPEC.md.

## 1. Goal
Developers, ops staff and students write cron expressions like `*/15 9-17 * * 1-5` and then cannot tell at a glance when the job will actually fire — mistakes surface as a backup that never ran or a job that ran every minute for a weekend. Success: the user pastes or builds a standard five-field cron expression, reads a precise plain-language explanation of each field and the whole schedule in their own language, and sees the next ten actual run times computed from now (or from a chosen start moment) in the browser's local timezone, which is named on screen. Invalid expressions fail with the exact field and token at fault, never with a silent wrong schedule.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Standard POSIX/Vixie five-field cron only: minute (0–59), hour (0–23), day-of-month (1–31), month (1–12 or JAN–DEC), day-of-week (0–7 with both 0 and 7 meaning Sunday, or SUN–SAT). Field syntax: `*`, single values, ranges `a-b`, lists `a,b,c`, steps `*/n`, `a-b/n` and `a/n` with the stated meaning; the classic DOM/DOW OR rule (when both day-of-month and day-of-week are restricted, a day matches if either matches) is implemented and explained in plain language because it is the single most misunderstood cron behaviour. Macros `@hourly`, `@daily`, `@midnight`, `@weekly`, `@monthly`, `@yearly`, `@annually` accepted as input and expanded visibly. Explicitly excluded and called out in help: seconds or year fields (six/seven-field Quartz expressions), Quartz-only tokens `?`, `L`, `W`, `#`, and actually scheduling or running anything. Next-run computation: next 10 firings strictly after the start moment, searching at most a stated number of years ahead (a schedule that can never fire — e.g. February 31st — must be reported as "no run found within the search window" with the reason explained, not loop forever or invent a date); day-of-month values that skip short months behave per cron semantics (the 31st simply does not fire in February). Builder mode: per-field controls (every / list of checkboxes / range / step) that compose the expression live, staying in sync two-way with the text field. All computation local; the timezone used is the browser's, displayed by name/offset.

Existing tools: Date Calculator does date differences and business days; Meeting Overlap compares people's availability; neither parses cron syntax, explains it, or enumerates future firings under the DOM/DOW rule. Initial catalog/spec/task scope contains no cron tool; fresh registry/path/draft check remains mandatory before admission. Fixture times are computed against fixed stated start moments, not "now", so tests are reproducible in any timezone the test pins.
