# Paint Coverage Calculator — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. Paint buying fails in predictable ways: people forget the fourth wall, subtract openings twice or not at all, divide by coverage before multiplying by coats, and — worst — round the litres down and run out mid-wall. This specification pins one exact area chain, keeps the can count a true mathematical ceiling (never rounded down, never padded when the litres divide exactly), and states the leftover instead of hiding it.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: anyone repainting a room at home, landlords refreshing a unit between tenants, and small contractors quoting a single-room job. The recurring problem: the coverage figure on the can (m²/L) is per coat, the room has openings that are not painted, and the answer has to come out in whole cans, not litres with decimals. Success is observable: the user enters a rectangular room's length, width and wall height, the counts of standard doors and windows (and any extra opening area), the number of coats and the paint's coverage, and receives the paintable area, the exact litres required, and — for a chosen can size — how many cans to buy and how much paint will be left over. An optional ceiling adds the ceiling to the same calculation. Non-goals: estimating for non-rectangular rooms, recommending brands or finishes, or pricing the job; the tool computes quantities from the figures entered.

Assumptions: a modern desktop or mobile browser; standard door and window sizes as defined in section 4 (users with non-standard openings use the custom area field); no network at runtime.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) room length and width (0.1–100 m) and wall height (0.1–10 m) for one rectangular room, four walls; (b) door count and window count (whole, 0–20 each) deducted at the fixed standard sizes of section 4; (c) a custom openings area (0–1,000 m²) for closets, archways and non-standard openings; (d) a ceiling toggle adding the ceiling area; (e) coats (whole, 1–4); (f) coverage (1–30 m²/L, the figure printed on the can, per coat); (g) a can size chosen from the closed list 0.5 / 1 / 2.5 / 5 / 10 L; (h) outputs: gross wall area, total deductions, paintable area, litres required, cans to buy, total volume purchased, and leftover litres. Excluded: L-shaped or multi-room projects; individually measured walls; sloped ceilings and stairwells; primer as a separate product with its own coverage; surface texture, porosity or sprayer-loss adjustments (the explainer tells the user the can's coverage already assumes a standard surface, and real coverage varies); prices, brands and colour.
Distinction from existing work: Tile Estimator counts discrete floor tiles and grout for a floor; this tool computes a liquid quantity for walls (and optionally a ceiling) from a coverage rate and rounds the purchase up to whole cans — different surface, different unit, different rounding question. Box Fit tests whether rigid items fit a box volume; Cut Planner optimises cuts on flat stock. Neither touches area-minus-openings × coats ÷ coverage. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time computes paint quantity; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: room 4 × 5 m, height 2.8 m, 1 door, 2 windows, custom openings 0, ceiling off, 2 coats, coverage 10 m²/L, can size 5 L — fixture P1 is already displayed (paintable 44.91 m², 8.98 L, 2 cans, leftover 1.02 L). Editing any field recomputes all outputs together. Turning the ceiling on adds the ceiling area into the same paintable total (shown as its own line in the area breakdown, so the user can see where every square metre came from — gross walls, minus openings, plus ceiling).
Error states: each field validates independently (empty, non-numeric, out of bounds, more than two decimal places, non-whole counts/coats) with outputs frozen at the last valid state marked "out of date". One computation-level error is distinct: if total deductions reach or exceed the gross wall area, the outputs are replaced by the explicit message that the openings are larger than the wall area and the inputs should be checked — never a zero or negative paint figure. Nothing persists across reload; no input appears in the URL or title.

## 4. Computation rules (normative)
All inputs are decimal numbers with at most two decimal places; all arithmetic is exact rational arithmetic; the only roundings are display (round-half-up to 2 dp) and the can ceiling below.
Areas: gross_wall = 2 × (L + W) × H. Standard deductions: one door = 0.9 × 2.1 = exactly 1.89 m²; one window = 1.5 × 1.2 = exactly 1.8 m². deductions = 1.89 × doors + 1.8 × windows + custom_openings. If deductions ≥ gross_wall the section 3 computation error applies (the ceiling, when on, does not rescue walls: the comparison is against gross_wall alone). paintable = gross_wall − deductions + (L × W if ceiling on, else 0).
Paint: litres_exact = paintable × coats ÷ coverage. Displayed litres = litres_exact rounded half-up to 2 dp.
Purchase: cans = the smallest whole number with cans × can_size ≥ litres_exact — the exact mathematical ceiling; when litres_exact is an exact multiple of can_size, cans = litres_exact ÷ can_size exactly (P3 asserts no phantom extra can). purchased = cans × can_size (always a whole multiple of the can size, displayed exactly). leftover_exact = purchased − litres_exact; displayed leftover is leftover_exact rounded half-up to 2 dp — note the displayed leftover is computed from the exact litres, so it may differ by 0.01 from purchased − displayed litres, and the tests assert the exact-derived value.
Display: every area and litre figure at 2 dp half-up; cans as a whole number.
Worked fixtures (normative, re-derived by script during drafting):
- P1: 4 × 5 m, H 2.8, 1 door, 2 windows, ceiling off, 2 coats, coverage 10, can 5 → gross **50.40 m²**, deductions **5.49 m²**, paintable **44.91 m²**, litres exact 8.982 → **8.98 L**, cans **2**, purchased 10 L, leftover **1.02 L** (exact 1.018).
- P2: P1 with ceiling on → paintable **64.91 m²**, litres exact 12.982 → **12.98 L**, cans **3**, purchased 15 L, leftover **2.02 L** (exact 2.018).
- P3: 3 × 3 m, H 2.5, no openings, ceiling off, 1 coat, coverage 10, can 1 → paintable **30.00 m²**, litres exactly **3.00 L**, cans **3**, leftover **0.00 L**.
- P4: 2 × 2 m, H 2.5 (gross 20.00 m²), custom openings 20 → the section 3 computation error (deductions ≥ gross), no paint figures.
- P5: 2.5 × 3 m, H 2.4, 1 window, 1 coat, coverage 12, can 2.5 → gross **26.40 m²**, paintable **24.60 m²**, litres **2.05 L** exact, cans **1**, leftover **0.45 L**.

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (area chain, litres, ceiling-division purchase — all exact rationals) with zero DOM access; a controller; a thin DOM layer. The standard door/window sizes, bounds, coat range and can-size list appear once as auditable constants matching section 4. Runtime dependencies: none.
Privacy/security: no network, no storage, no analytics; inputs render as text only; no eval/Function; no external resources.
Honesty rules (normative, testable): the page states that the standard door/window sizes are assumptions and non-standard openings belong in the custom area field; that coverage is the can manufacturer's figure for a standard surface and real coverage varies with texture and application; and that the leftover is paint remaining after buying whole cans — the tool never presents the rounded-down litres as sufficient.
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI, including the area breakdown lines, the computation error and the explainer. Inputs are native and labelled; the ceiling toggle is a real checkbox/switch with a text label; outputs are text with a visible breakdown (never colour alone); keyboard-complete flow; recomputed outputs announced politely as text.
Performance bounds: a few rational operations per edit. Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs offline from file:// or any static host.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages; fixtures P1–P5 reproduced exactly, including the area breakdown, the ceiling variant and the computation error.
- boundaries: dimensions at their exact bounds accepted and just outside refused; counts 0 and 20 accepted, 21 and non-whole refused; coats 1 and 4 accepted, 5 refused; deductions exactly equal to gross walls trigger the error (P4), deductions one cent of a square metre below gross produce a valid tiny area.
- privacy: zero network after load; no storage; inputs absent from URL/title and never transmitted.
- security: field content containing markup renders inert; no external resources; no eval/Function.
- usability: a first-time user painting a bedroom gets a can count without instructions and can explain where the leftover figure comes from (spot-check).
- accessibility: labelled native inputs and toggle; the breakdown and error readable as text; keyboard-complete; errors textually associated with their fields.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent (including breakdown lines, computation error and explainer); correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output on Node 24; the standard opening sizes, bounds and can list appear once as auditable constants matching section 4.
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop and mobile-width layout; identical outputs for the fixture corpus across browsers.

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests with independently derived expectations: (a) fixtures P1–P5 exactly — every intermediate (gross, deductions, paintable) and final (litres, cans, purchased, leftover) value; (b) the ceiling rule: exact multiples buy no extra can (P3), one microlitre over a multiple buys one more (litres_exact = 3.001 at can 1 → 4 cans constructed via inputs or core-level case); (c) the error rule: deductions equal to gross, deductions above gross, and deductions just below gross (valid) — including that an enabled ceiling does not change the comparison; (d) bounds per field at and beyond each limit; counts/coats whole-only; (e) leftover asserted from exact litres (a case where purchased − displayed litres would give a different cent than the exact-derived leftover).
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): fixtures entered through the real UI in both languages; ceiling toggle on/off updating the breakdown; the computation error appearing and clearing when inputs are fixed; stale-state behaviour on an invalid field. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
Explicitly NOT_RUN at specification stage: all product tests above; this document claims document completeness only.
