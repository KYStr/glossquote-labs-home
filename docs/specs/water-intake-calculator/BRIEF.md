# Daily Water Intake Calculator — preparatory BRIEF v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. Full normative contract is SPEC.md.

## 1. Goal
"How much water should I drink a day" is one of the most searched health questions, and the honest answer is a range that depends on body size, activity and climate — not the single magic number ("eight glasses") that most pages print. This tool computes that range from a stated, inspectable model: a weight-based baseline of 30–35 ml per kilogram, flat additions for activity level, and a climate multiplier, applied in a fixed order, in exact arithmetic. Its honesty is the product: the model and its constants are printed on the page, the result is always a range, and the page carries a permanent not-medical-advice statement — no diagnosis language, no disease or condition inputs, no claim that the figure is anyone's medical requirement. Success: two people with different weights, activity and climate get visibly different ranges that match the hand-worked fixtures exactly, and a reader can recompute any result from the constants shown. No account, no health record, nothing stored.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Pinned in the SPEC: inputs are body weight (20–300 kg, 0.1 steps), activity level (sedentary +0 ml, light +350, moderate +700, high +1,050 — flat additions applied to both ends of the baseline), and climate (temperate ×1.0, warm ×1.1, hot ×1.25). Model (normative, in this order): low = (30 × kg + activity) × climate; high = (35 × kg + activity) × climate; computed exactly in decimal arithmetic and displayed rounded half-up to whole millilitres, plus a cups equivalent (ml ÷ 250, one decimal, half-up). The three constants sets (30/35 ml per kg, the activity additions, the climate multipliers) ship as one auditable data block and are printed on the page as the model. Excluded: age, sex, pregnancy, illness, medication or any other health-condition input (each changes needs in ways a static page must not model); food-water accounting; hourly or per-workout schedules; reminders to drink; any "you are dehydrated" style assessment of the user. The result is labelled a general educational estimate range, with the not-medical-advice note adjacent to the result, not buried.

Existing tools: BMI/TDEE Calculator computes body-mass index and calorie expenditure from personal metrics — different quantity (energy, not fluid), different model, and it likewise avoids diagnosis. Running Pace, Electricity Cost and the other calculators share only generic form arithmetic. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task computes water intake; fresh registry/path/draft check remains mandatory before admission. All fixtures — 70 kg sedentary temperate → 2,100–2,450 ml (8.4–9.8 cups); 70 kg moderate temperate → 2,800–3,150 ml; 60 kg light warm → 2,365–2,695 ml; 80 kg high hot → 4,312.5–4,812.5 → 4,313–4,813 ml — are re-derived by script during drafting.
