# Running Pace Calculator — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. Pace arithmetic looks trivial and fails in the seams: a split table whose rounded rows do not add up, a time typed as `45:90` silently reinterpreted, a race prediction presented as a promise. This specification pins the time grammar, defines split segments from the displayed cumulatives so the printed table is always self-consistent, and fences the Riegel prediction inside its stated assumptions.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: runners and walkers planning workouts and races, coaches setting target paces, and beginners converting a finish time into "how fast was each kilometre, actually". The recurring problem: many calculators solve only pace-from-time-and-distance, display split tables whose rounded numbers contradict the total, accept ambiguous time text and guess, or extrapolate race times with an unnamed formula and no limits. Success is observable: the user chooses which of pace, time or distance to solve for, fills the other two in kilometres or miles, and gets the solved value, an even-pace split table that adds up exactly as printed, and — from the resulting time and distance as a known performance — Riegel predictions for 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon, each shown with its predicted pace and the model's assumptions. Non-goals: GPS, maps, elevation, training plans, heart-rate or power zones, negative-split strategies, and any implication that a prediction is a guarantee — the page MUST carry the prediction limits where the predictions appear.

Assumptions: a modern desktop browser; even pacing for splits (stated); no network at runtime.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) the three-direction solver — pace from time + distance, time from pace + distance, distance from time + pace — selected by an explicit solve-for control; (b) a distance-unit toggle (kilometres / miles) applying to the distance field, the pace unit (per km / per mile) and the split markers together; (c) the split table of section 4; (d) the Riegel prediction table of section 4 for the four fixed target distances, using the currently solved time and distance as the known result; (e) copy-free plain text output (no sharing affordance). Accepted bounds for typed inputs: distance 0.01–300 of the chosen unit; total time 1 second – 99:59:59; pace 0:30–59:59 per unit. These bounds bind typed inputs only; a computed result is always displayed as computed, even when it falls outside the typed-input bounds (for example a very fast computed pace over a very short distance). Excluded: per-lap track splits other than the unit splits defined, elevation or weather adjustment, saved performances, and predictions for targets beyond the marathon or below 1 km.
Distinction from existing work: the Metronome specification schedules audio clicks at a musical tempo in BPM; it has no distance or duration-solving semantics. Date Calculator performs calendar arithmetic; Focus Timer counts down; Work Hours sums worked shifts; Event Timeline lays out programme times. None solves the pace/time/distance triangle or applies a race-prediction model. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time does so; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: solve-for = pace; time `55:00`, distance 10 km prefilled; pace 5:30/km, the 10-row split table and the prediction table are computed and visible. Changing the solve-for selector moves the solved field into a read-only output rendering (its previous text is kept as the starting input when it becomes an input again — switching direction never discards the user's numbers). Editing any input recomputes the output, splits and predictions together.
Error states are per-field and specific: malformed time text (the expected grammar is restated in the error: total seconds, M:SS, or H:MM:SS with trailing components 00–59); a component ≥60 named; distance zero, negative or above 300; pace zero. While any input is invalid, the output, splits and predictions freeze at the last valid state marked "out of date". Distance-unit switching converts the distance value and pace display from the unrounded internal store (seconds and kilometres), never from the rounded display text. Nothing persists across reload; performances never appear in the URL or title.

## 4. Rules (normative)
Time grammar (one grammar for the time field and the pace field; normative): after trimming ASCII whitespace, the text MUST match one of — (i) digits only: total seconds; (ii) `M:SS`: one or more digits, a colon, exactly two digits 00–59; (iii) `H:MM:SS`: one or more digits, then two components of exactly two digits each, 00–59. Anything else (one-digit seconds after a colon, `45:90`, decimal points, signs, words like `5min`) is the malformed-time error. Display format: totals under one hour as `M:SS` (minutes not zero-padded, seconds zero-padded to two); one hour or more as `H:MM:SS`. All displayed times and paces are rounded to the nearest whole second, half away from zero.
Internal model: time in seconds (real), distance in kilometres (real). Conversion: 1 mile = 1.609344 km exactly; pace per mile in seconds = pace per km × 1.609344. Inputs bind: distance as typed in the chosen unit, converted unrounded; pace as typed per chosen unit, converted to seconds per km unrounded.
Solving: pace_s_per_km = time_s ÷ distance_km; time_s = pace_s_per_km × distance_km; distance_km = time_s ÷ pace_s_per_km. Solved distance displays in the chosen unit to two decimals (half away from zero). Solved pace displays per chosen unit. Solved time displays per the display format.
Split table: markers at each whole unit of the chosen distance unit — step 1 when the whole-unit count is ≤100, otherwise step 5 — plus a finish row at the exact distance when the distance is not itself a marker. Row content: marker distance (in the chosen unit; integers for step rows, the exact distance with up to two decimals for the finish row, labelled "finish"), segment time, cumulative time. Cumulative at a marker = round(pace_s_per_unit × marker) to whole seconds. **Segment times are the differences of successive displayed (rounded) cumulative times**, the first segment being the first cumulative; the finish segment likewise. Consequence, stated on the page: the printed segments always sum exactly to the printed total. Even pace is assumed and stated in the table heading.
Riegel predictions: for each target distance D₂ in {5 km, 10 km, 21.0975 km, 42.195 km}: T₂ = T₁ × (D₂/D₁)^1.06, where (T₁, D₁) is the current solved time and distance in km. Predicted time rounds to whole seconds; each row also shows the predicted pace per chosen unit. The row whose target equals D₁ exactly (when it does) shows the actual time, marked "your input". The assumptions block beside the table states: the formula assumes endurance training appropriate to the target, similar terrain and weather, and comparable effort; it is most reliable when the target is within roughly half to double the known distance, and predictions are estimates, not promises. No prediction is computed for a target beyond 42.195 km.
Worked fixtures (normative, re-derived by script during drafting):
- A: time 55:00 (3300 s), distance 10 km → pace **5:30/km**; the same run per mile: 330 × 1.609344 = 531.08 s → **8:51/mile**.
- B: pace 5:00/km (300 s), distance 21.0975 km → time = 6329.25 s → **1:45:29**.
- C: time 3:30:00 (12,600 s), pace 6:00/km (360 s) → distance **35.00 km**.
- Splits for fixture A extended to 10.5 km at 330 s/km: cumulatives km 1–10 = 5:30, 11:00, 16:30, 22:00, 27:30, 33:00, 38:30, 44:00, 49:30, 55:00; finish 10.5 → cumulative **57:45**, finish segment **2:45**; segments sum to 57:45 exactly.
- Riegel from 10 km in 45:00 (2700 s): 5K → 1295.01 s → **21:35** (pace 4:19/km); 10K → **45:00** (your input); half marathon → 5957.29 s → **1:39:17** (4:42/km); marathon → 12420.54 s → **3:27:01** (4:54/km).

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (time parser/formatter, unit conversion, solver, split builder working from rounded cumulatives, Riegel predictor) with zero DOM access; a controller; a thin DOM layer. Constants (mile factor, Riegel exponent 1.06, the four target distances) appear once as auditable data matching section 4. Runtime dependencies: none.
Privacy/security: no network, no storage, no analytics; inputs render as text only; no eval/Function; no external resources.
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI, including the even-pace statement, the segment-rounding sentence and the Riegel assumptions. Splits and predictions are real tables; the solved value is text; all controls native and labelled; keyboard-complete flow; errors associated with their fields.
Performance bounds: at most 101 split rows (step rule) and 4 predictions; trivial arithmetic per keystroke. Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs offline from file:// or any static host.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages and both distance units; all three solve directions; splits and predictions recomputed together and matching section 4 fixtures exactly; solve-for switching preserves the user's numbers.
- boundaries: time grammar edges (digits-only seconds; `5:30` valid; `5:3`, `45:90`, `1:60:00` refused with the grammar restated); distance 0.01 and 300 accepted, 0 and 300.01 refused; unit count 100 → step 1, 101 → step 5 with a finish row; a non-integer distance's finish row correct.
- privacy: zero network after load; no storage; performances absent from URL/title.
- security: field text containing markup renders inert; no external resources; no eval/Function.
- usability: a runner solves a workout pace, reads the split for kilometre 7 without instructions, and can state from the assumptions block what a prediction does and does not promise (spot-check).
- accessibility: tables are real tables with headers; solved value and assumptions as text; labelled native controls; keyboard-complete; errors associated with fields.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent (including even-pace, rounding and assumption statements); correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output on Node 24; constants appear once as auditable data matching section 4.
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop; identical outputs for the fixture corpus across browsers.

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests with independently derived expectations: (a) fixtures A, B and C exactly (both pace units for A); (b) the full split table of the splits fixture, asserting every cumulative, the finish segment, and that printed segments sum to the printed total; a constructed uneven-rounding case (a pace whose per-km product lands on.5 s) proving segments come from rounded cumulatives; (c) Riegel: all four predictions of the fixture, the "your input" marking at exact equality, and no rows beyond the marathon; (d) time grammar: every accepted shape and every refusal class in section 6 boundaries; (e) unit behaviour: switching km ↔ miles converts from the unrounded store (a round trip returns identical displayed outputs) and re-expresses pace and splits in the other unit; (f) solve-for switching preserves values across a full cycle (pace → time → distance → pace).
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): fixtures entered through the real UI in both languages and both units; stale-state behaviour on a malformed time; the step-5 split table for a 120 km input shown with a finish row. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
Explicitly NOT_RUN at specification stage: all product tests above; this document claims document completeness only.
