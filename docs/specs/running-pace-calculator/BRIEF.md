# Running Pace Calculator — preparatory BRIEF v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. Full normative contract is SPEC.md.

## 1. Goal
Runners, walkers and their coaches juggle the same triangle every week — pace, time, distance — and most calculators solve only one direction, hide their rounding in the split table, or offer race predictions with no formula and no limits. Success: the user fills in any two of pace, time and distance and gets the third, in kilometres or miles; sees an even-pace split table whose rows visibly add up; and can read predicted times for 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon from a known result, computed by the named Riegel formula with its assumptions and limits printed next to the numbers. No account, no upload, no GPS, no saved history: the numbers never leave the page.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Pinned in the SPEC: a three-field solver with an explicit solve-for selector (the solved field is an output, never simultaneously an input); one closed time grammar (plain digits = total seconds; `M:SS`; `H:MM:SS`, trailing components 00–59) shared by the time field and the pace field; distance as a decimal in km or miles (0.01–300 of the chosen unit) with exact conversion 1 mile = 1.609344 km; all internal arithmetic in unrounded seconds and kilometres, displays rounded half away from zero (times and paces to whole seconds as M:SS or H:MM:SS, solved distance to 2 decimals). The split table is normative about rounding honesty: rows at each whole distance unit (step 1, or step 5 when the distance exceeds 100 units) plus a finish row for a final partial unit, segment times defined as differences of the displayed cumulative times so the table always adds up exactly as printed. Race prediction uses Riegel T₂ = T₁·(D₂/D₁)^1.06 against four fixed target distances (5 km, 10 km, 21.0975 km, 42.195 km), predictions only, never for targets beyond the marathon, with the comparability assumptions (similar terrain, weather, and endurance training; reliability fades as the distance ratio grows) stated in the UI. Excluded: GPS or map input, elevation, training plans, heart-rate zones, negative splits or any non-even pacing strategy, per-400 m track splits, saving results, and sharing.

Existing tools: the Metronome specification is a WebAudio click scheduler for musical tempo (BPM) — it shares the word "pace" in English slang and nothing else: no distance, no duration solving. Date Calculator and Focus Timer handle calendar days and countdowns; Work Hours sums shifts; Event Timeline lays out schedules. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task solves the pace/time/distance triangle or applies the Riegel formula; fresh registry/path/draft check remains mandatory before admission. All fixtures — the three solve directions, the 10.5 km split table, both per-km and per-mile paces of the same run, and the four Riegel predictions from a 45:00 10K — are re-derived by script during drafting.
