# Fuel Cost Calculator — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. Trip fuel maths fails quietly in two places: sloppy conversion constants (a US gallon is not an Imperial gallon, and "MPG" without a country is meaningless) and money rounded too early, so the total on screen disagrees with the same inputs computed elsewhere. This specification pins exact rational constants, one normalisation path (everything becomes litres and kilometres), and a single rounding point at display.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: drivers budgeting a road trip, families splitting a shared car's fuel bill, delivery and field-service drivers estimating a job's fuel cost, and anyone whose car quotes consumption in a unit different from the one fuel is sold in. The recurring problem: distance in miles, consumption in UK MPG, price per litre — three unit systems in one question, and mental arithmetic that conflates US and Imperial gallons or rounds at every step. Success is observable: the user enters distance (km or miles), consumption (L/100km, km/L, MPG US or MPG UK), and a fuel price (per litre, per US gallon or per Imperial gallon, in a chosen display currency), and receives fuel needed in litres, the total cost, the consumption echoed in L/100km, and — optionally — a per-person share when splitting among the car's occupants. Non-goals: routing, vehicle advice, or any claim about what a car "should" consume; the tool computes from the figures entered and says so.

Assumptions: a modern desktop or mobile browser; the user knows or can look up their car's consumption figure; no network at runtime; the currency selector is a display label only — the tool never converts between currencies.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) distance input with unit selector km / miles; (b) consumption input with unit selector L/100km / km/L / MPG (US) / MPG (UK) — the two MPG options are always labelled with their country, never a bare "MPG"; (c) fuel price input with a per-unit selector (per litre / per US gallon / per Imperial gallon) and a currency selector (NT$, US$, €, £, ¥) that changes only the displayed symbol; (d) an optional split: a toggle plus a whole-number people count 1–12, producing a per-person share; (e) outputs: fuel needed in litres, total cost, per-person share when the split is on, and the entered consumption normalised and echoed in L/100km so the user can sanity-check the conversion. Excluded: maps, routes and multi-leg trips; vehicle model databases; tolls, parking and other trip costs; electric-vehicle charging; tip and tax; saving, history or accounts.
Distinction from existing work: Bill Splitter divides a total the user already knows among people, with tip/tax — it never computes a total; here computing the total from distance × consumption × price is the primary function, and the split is a secondary display of that computed total with no tip/tax. Unit Converter converts one value between units and has no concept of fuel, price or a trip. Electricity Cost Calculator prices electricity from watts × hours × tariff; this tool prices liquid fuel from distance × consumption × pump price. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time computes trip fuel cost; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: distance 250, unit km; consumption 6.5, unit L/100km; price 1.80 per litre, currency US$; split off — the outputs already show fixture F1 (16.25 L, $29.25, echo 6.50 L/100km). Editing any field or selector recomputes all outputs together. Switching a unit selector never silently rescales the typed number: the number stays as typed and is reinterpreted in the new unit (the page's labels make the current unit unambiguous at all times; this rule is stated in the explainer so a switch is never mistaken for a conversion of the value).
The split toggle reveals the people count (default 4); turning it off hides the per-person output and nothing else. When the split is on, the rounding note ("each share is rounded to the nearest cent; shares may add up a cent or two differently from the total") is visible next to the share.
Error states: each numeric field validates independently — empty, non-numeric, zero or negative, more than two decimal places (people count: non-whole), or above its section 4 cap — the field carries its error and the outputs freeze at the last valid state marked "out of date". Nothing persists across reload; no input appears in the URL or title.

## 4. Computation rules (normative)
Exact constants (definitions, not measurements): 1 mile = 1.609344 km; 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 L; 1 Imperial gallon = 4.54609 L. The core MUST carry these as exact decimals/fractions and derive: L/100km from km/L as 100 ÷ (km/L); from MPG(US) as (112903/480) ÷ MPG — since 100 × 3.785411784 / 1.609344 = 235.21458333… exactly the fraction 112903/480; from MPG(UK) as (56826125/201168) ÷ MPG — since 100 × 4.54609 / 1.609344 = 282.48093633… exactly that fraction. Decimal approximations of these constants in the core are a defect.
Inputs and bounds: distance d, 0 < d ≤ 100,000 in the entered unit, at most two decimal places; consumption c by unit — L/100km: 0.1 ≤ c ≤ 100; km/L: 1 ≤ c ≤ 100; MPG(US) and MPG(UK): 1 ≤ c ≤ 200 — at most two decimal places; price p, 0 < p ≤ 10,000 per chosen volume unit, at most two decimal places; people n whole, 1–12, only used when the split is on.
Normalisation: distance_km = d × 1.609344 when miles, else d. consumption_L100 = per the identities above (L/100km input used as-is). price_per_L = p ÷ 3.785411784 when per US gallon, p ÷ 4.54609 when per Imperial gallon, else p.
Results (exact rational arithmetic end to end; no intermediate rounding): fuel_L = distance_km × consumption_L100 ÷ 100. total = fuel_L × price_per_L. share = total ÷ n when the split is on.
Display rounding (the only rounding in the tool, round-half-up applied once per displayed number): fuel in litres to 2 dp; total and share to 2 dp (cents); the consumption echo in L/100km to 2 dp. The share is computed from the exact total, never from the displayed total — so displayed shares × n may differ from the displayed total by a cent or two, and the section 3 note says exactly that.
Worked fixtures (normative, re-derived by script during drafting):
- F1: 250 km, 6.5 L/100km, $1.80/L, split 4 → fuel **16.25 L**, total **$29.25**, echo 6.50 L/100km, share **$7.31** (exact 7.3125).
- F2: 100 miles, 40 MPG(US), $3.60 per US gallon → distance 160.9344 km, echo **5.88 L/100km** (exact 5.8803645833…), fuel exactly 2.5 US gallons = **9.46 L** displayed (exact 9.46352946), total **$9.00**.
- F3: 300 km, 15 km/L, NT$30.50/L → fuel **20.00 L**, total **NT$610.00**, echo **6.67 L/100km** (exact 6.666…).
- F4: 200 miles, 50 MPG(UK), £1.45/L → fuel exact 4 Imperial gallons = 18.18436 L, displayed **18.18 L**, total **£26.37** (exact 26.367322), echo **5.65 L/100km** (exact 5.6496187266…).
- F5: 100 km, 10 L/100km, $1.00/L, split 3 → fuel **10.00 L**, total **$10.00**, share **$3.33**, and 3 × 3.33 = 9.99 ≠ 10.00 — the rounding note case.

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (unit normalisation and the three results as exact rationals — BigInt fractions or equivalent exact decimal arithmetic — plus a display formatter that is the only place rounding happens) with zero DOM access; a controller; a thin DOM layer. The three defining constants, the two derived MPG fractions, the per-unit bounds and the currency list appear once as auditable data matching section 4. Runtime dependencies: none; floating-point arithmetic in the core is a defect (doubles cannot represent the F2 exactness the fixtures assert).
Privacy/security: no network, no storage, no analytics; inputs render as text only; no eval/Function; no external resources.
Honesty rules (normative, testable): the page states that results are estimates from the consumption figure entered (real consumption varies with driving), that the currency selector changes the symbol only and performs no exchange conversion, and that US and UK gallons differ — the two MPG options and two gallon price options are never merged or left unlabelled.
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI, including unit labels with countries, the rounding note and the honesty explainer. Every selector is a native labelled control; outputs are text (never colour alone); keyboard-complete flow; recomputed outputs announced politely as text.
Performance bounds: a handful of rational operations per edit. Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs offline from file:// or any static host.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages; all four consumption units, both distance units and all three price units exercised; fixtures F1–F5 reproduced exactly, including echoes and shares.
- boundaries: distance 0 and 100,000.01 refused, 100,000 accepted; consumption at each unit's exact bounds accepted and just outside refused; price 0 refused; people 0, 13 and 2.5 refused; the F5 share-rounding divergence shown with its note.
- privacy: zero network after load; no storage; inputs absent from URL/title and never transmitted.
- security: field content containing markup renders inert; no external resources; no eval/Function.
- usability: a driver with a UK-MPG car and a per-litre price gets a trip cost without instructions and can point to the L/100km echo confirming the conversion (spot-check).
- accessibility: all selectors native and labelled; outputs as text; keyboard-complete; errors textually associated with their fields.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent (including country-labelled gallons, the rounding note and explainer); correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output on Node 24; the exact constants and derived fractions appear once as auditable data matching section 4; no floating-point core.
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop and mobile-width layout; identical outputs for the fixture corpus across browsers.

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests with independently derived expectations: (a) fixtures F1–F5 exactly — fuel, total, echo and share, including F2's exact 2.5-gallon identity and F5's 9.99 ≠ 10.00 divergence; (b) constant identities: 40 MPG(US) → 5.8803645833… L/100km and 15 km/L → 6.666… L/100km asserted to at least 10 significant digits, plus a US-vs-UK discrimination case (same MPG number, both countries, different fuel — 40 MPG US vs UK at 100 miles differ and both are asserted); (c) bounds: every section 4 cap accepted at the value and refused beyond it, per unit; more-than-two-decimal inputs refused; people non-whole refused; (d) the share is asserted to derive from the exact total (a case where rounding the total first would give a different share cent).
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): fixtures entered through the real UI in both languages; unit-switch reinterpretation behaviour observed (number unchanged, label changed, outputs recomputed); split toggle on/off; stale-state behaviour on an invalid field. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
Explicitly NOT_RUN at specification stage: all product tests above; this document claims document completeness only.
