# Mortgage Amortization Calculator — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. This is a planning tool: it MUST display, in both languages, that results are reproducible estimates from user-typed figures, not a bank quotation, approval, or financial advice.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: home buyers and refinancing borrowers in Taiwan comparing loan offers and repayment strategies. The recurring problem: bank quotations show a monthly payment but hide how grace periods, two-tranche rates and early repayments change total interest; generic online calculators disagree with each other because their rounding rules are unstated, and using them means typing personal finances into marketing sites. Success is observable: the user enters principal, annual rate(s), term, and an optional grace period, receives a month-by-month amortization ledger that any third party can re-derive by hand from the formulas and rounding rules in section 4, adds early-repayment events to see interest saved against the untouched baseline, and exports the exact visible schedule as CSV. Non-goals: bank or product comparison databases, live rate lookup, fees/insurance/tax computation, credit-card or revolving credit, investment projections, affordability underwriting, saved profiles, and any claim that a bank will use identical rounding.

Assumptions: a modern desktop browser; figures are planning numbers typed by the user; amounts are New Taiwan dollars by default label but the arithmetic is currency-neutral; no network at runtime.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) two repayment methods — equal total payment (annuity) and equal principal; (b) term 1–40 years in months; (c) single annual rate, or an optional second tranche rate applying from a chosen month onward; (d) grace period 0–60 months (interest-only, capped at half the term, whichever is smaller MUST be enforced as an error, not silently clamped); (e) early-repayment events: a bounded list (max 12) of {month, amount, mode}, mode being reduce-term (payment unchanged, schedule shortens) or reduce-payment (term unchanged, payment recomputed); (f) outputs: monthly ledger (month index, opening balance, payment, interest part, principal part, closing balance), yearly roll-up, totals (total paid, total interest), and an interest-saved figure versus the same loan with no prepayment events; (g) CSV export of the monthly ledger exactly as displayed; (h) full reset.

Excluded: daily interest accrual, irregular payment days, floating-rate schedules beyond two tranches, negative-amortization products, fees, insurance, taxes, multiple loans, refinancing cost comparison, charts (a later specification may add them), persistence, accounts, network calls of any kind.

Distinction from existing work: Subscription Budget lists recurring charges and totals; it has no debt recurrence or time-indexed balances. Bill Splitter performs a one-shot division of a shared expense. Unit Price divides price by quantity. Grade Target solves a one-variable weighted average. None models month-indexed balances with grace periods, tranche switching or dated prepayment events; the recurrence and ledger output are distinct. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time computes a loan amortization schedule; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: annuity method, principal and rate empty, term 20 years (240 months), grace 0, single rate, no prepayment events; results area shows an instruction placeholder, never a sample schedule presented as the user's result.
Editing any loan field recomputes the full schedule deterministically after a short debounce (≤200 ms); invalid input shows a field-level error and the previous valid schedule is replaced by the error state — a stale schedule MUST NOT remain exportable next to an input error.
Prepayment events are added one at a time (month picker bounded to the current schedule length, amount, mode); each event row can be deleted; adding an event whose month is inside the grace period is allowed (it reduces balance during grace; interest after it is recomputed on the lower balance). Events at the same month combine in entry order.
Tranche rate: enabling the second rate reveals its rate and start-month fields; disabling removes its effect entirely. Changing the method (annuity/equal-principal) recomputes everything, including prepayment modes' effects, under the new method.
Export: the CSV button exports the currently displayed monthly ledger (post-prepayment scenario) and, when prepayments exist, the baseline totals are included in the summary header lines of the CSV as comments prefixed with `#`. Nothing is stored; leaving or reloading discards all inputs. Repeated calculation with identical inputs MUST produce identical numbers and identical CSV bytes (no timestamps inside the CSV body; the filename may carry a local date).

## 4. Input, arithmetic and exact output
Money representation: all arithmetic in integer cents (1/100 dollar). Inputs are decimal dollars with at most 2 fraction digits; principal MUST be > 0 and ≤ 10^12 cents; annual rate is a percentage with at most 4 fraction digits (e.g. 2.095), 0 ≤ rate ≤ 30; monthly rate = annualRate / 100 / 12 kept as an exact rational during each month's interest computation.
Interest for a month = round-half-up(openingBalanceCents × annualRatePercent / 1200) to integer cents, computed with exact integer arithmetic (numerator = balance × rateScaled; rateScaled = round(annualRatePercent × 10^4); interest = floor((2×numerator + 12000000) / 24000000) — an implementation MAY use any equivalent exact method, but results MUST match this rounding bit-for-bit).
Annuity payment: computed once per stable segment (after start, after a tranche switch, after a reduce-payment prepayment, after grace ends) as A = round-half-up(P × i / (1 − (1+i)^(−n))) where P is the segment opening balance in cents, i the exact monthly rate, n the remaining months of the segment; when i = 0, A = ceil(P / n) except the final payment takes the remainder. Each month: interest per the rule above; principal part = A − interest; closing = opening − principal part. Final month of a segment: payment = opening + interest (exact payoff), so the balance ends at exactly 0 and the final payment may differ from A by a few cents.
Equal-principal method: principal part per month = floor(P / n) with the remainder cents distributed one extra cent to each of the earliest months; payment = principal part + interest; the same interest rule applies.
Grace period: during grace months the payment equals that month's interest exactly and the principal part is 0; the amortization segment starts the month after grace ends with n = total months − grace months.
Tranche switch: at the switch month the new rate applies to that month's interest onward; for the annuity method the payment is recomputed at the switch over the then-remaining months and balance; for equal-principal the principal parts are NOT recomputed (only interest changes), unless a reduce-payment prepayment also occurs.
Prepayment: at the event month, after that month's regular payment, the event amount is subtracted from the balance (an event larger than the post-payment balance is an error naming the maximum). Reduce-term: payment unchanged; the schedule ends when the balance reaches 0, with a final adjusted payment as above. Reduce-payment: payment recomputed over the remaining months at the current rate. Baseline comparison: the same inputs with the event list emptied; interest saved = baseline total interest − scenario total interest (may be 0, never negative in display; if a defect would make it negative, that is a bug, not a display case).
Worked fixture (normative): principal 120,000.00, annual rate 12%, 3 months, annuity, no grace: A = 40,803.45; month 1 interest 1,200.00, principal 39,603.45, closing 80,396.55; month 2 interest 803.97, principal 39,999.48, closing 40,397.07; month 3 interest 403.97, final payment 40,801.04, closing 0. Total interest 2,407.94. A zero-rate fixture: principal 10,000.00, 0%, 4 months ⇒ payments 2,500.00 ×4, interest 0.
Outputs formatting: amounts displayed with thousands separators and exactly 2 decimals; the ledger shows at most the full term (480 rows) with a year roll-up summary above it; CSV columns exactly: month,opening,payment,interest,principal,closing with raw cent-exact decimal values (no thousands separators) and a trailing newline; encoding UTF-8 without BOM; filename `amortization-<yyyymmdd>.csv` containing no input figures.

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (schedule engine over integer cents, CSV builder, formatting helpers) with zero DOM access; a controller binding form state to the core; a thin DOM layer rendering the ledger (virtualisation is unnecessary at ≤480 rows; a plain table is required for accessibility). Runtime dependencies: none; the engine MUST NOT use binary floating point for money anywhere in the pipeline (floats permitted only, if at all, inside the annuity factor before the single specified rounding, and the result MUST still match the integer fixtures).
Privacy/security: no network, no storage, no analytics; figures never enter URLs, titles or filenames; table cells are text nodes, not markup built from user strings; CSV is generated as a Blob and the object URL is revoked after download.
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI with identical fields, errors and table headers; the ledger is a real `<table>` with `<th scope>` headers; form errors are tied to fields with `aria-describedby` and announced politely; every control is keyboard reachable; the planning-estimate disclaimer appears above the results in both languages.
Performance bounds: full recompute of 480 months with 12 events MUST complete within 50 ms on a mid-range laptop. Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout shared with sibling tools (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs offline from file:// or any static host.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages; both methods; grace, tranche and prepayment flows; CSV matches the displayed ledger cell-for-cell; totals and interest-saved agree with the ledger.
- boundaries: zero rate; 1-month term; grace at its cap and one month beyond (error); prepayment equal to the balance (schedule ends) and one cent above (error); 40-year maximum term; rate with 4 fraction digits.
- privacy: zero network requests after load; no storage; inputs absent from filename, URL and title; CSV contains only schedule data and the specified summary comments.
- security: no markup injection via any field; validated numeric parsing (no `eval`, no implicit string arithmetic); Blob URL revoked.
- usability: a first-time user can reproduce the section 4 worked fixture and understand why the final payment differs; errors name the field, the limit and the entered value.
- accessibility: real table semantics, labelled controls, announced errors, keyboard-only completion of the full flow including export.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent, including the disclaimer; correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output from a clean checkout on Node 24; fixtures in tests are the published ones from section 4 plus independently derived additions.
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop; CSV opens identically in Excel, LibreOffice and Google Sheets (decimal point, no separator ambiguity).

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests with independently derived expectations: (a) the two section 4 fixtures exactly, cent for cent; (b) a 12-month annuity schedule checked against an independent spreadsheet-style recomputation frozen as literal rows; (c) equal-principal remainder-cent distribution; (d) grace-period interest-only rows and the post-grace recomputation; (e) tranche switch month interest and annuity recomputation; (f) prepayment reduce-term shortening and reduce-payment recomputation, same-month event ordering, over-balance error; (g) interest rounding half-up edge cases (exact .5 cent); (h) CSV byte equality against a frozen expected file for the worked fixture.
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): enter the worked fixture and compare the rendered table to the fixture; trigger each error state; export and re-import the CSV into a spreadsheet to confirm parsing; keyboard-only completion; both languages screenshotted. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
Explicitly NOT_RUN at specification stage: all product tests above; this document claims document completeness only.
