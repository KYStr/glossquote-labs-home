# Mortgage Amortization Calculator — preparatory BRIEF v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. Full normative contract is SPEC.md.

## 1. Goal
A home buyer or refinancing borrower in Taiwan needs to see, before signing, exactly how a loan's monthly payment is composed and how much interest a grace period, a rate change between loan tranches, or partial early repayments will cost or save — without uploading their finances to a bank marketing site or a spreadsheet template they cannot verify. Success: enter principal, annual rate(s), term and grace period; receive a deterministic month-by-month amortization table whose every row can be re-derived by hand from the stated formulas; model early repayments and see the interest saved; export the table as CSV. All arithmetic is local and reproducible to the cent.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Equal-total-payment (annuity) amortization as the primary method with equal-principal as a selectable alternative; single-rate loans plus an optional two-tranche rate structure (first N years at rate A, remainder at rate B); grace period (interest-only) up to 5 years; monthly schedule with opening balance, payment, principal part, interest part and closing balance; yearly summary; early repayment events (lump sum at a chosen month, with reduce-payment or reduce-term choice) entered as a bounded list; totals (total paid, total interest) and interest-saved comparison against the no-prepayment baseline; CSV export of the visible schedule. No bank-specific fee/insurance calculations, no credit-card revolving interest, no investment return projection, no currency conversion, no saved profiles, no network rate lookup — rates are typed by the user.

Existing tools: Subscription Budget inventories recurring charges; Bill Splitter settles shared expenses once; Unit Price compares shelf prices; Grade Target works backwards from scores. None builds a time-indexed debt schedule with grace periods, tranche rates or prepayment events; the input model (loan terms + dated events), the recurrence arithmetic and the output (a full amortization ledger) are distinct. Financial figures are user-typed planning numbers, and the page must state it is a planning estimate, not a bank quote or financial advice; rounding follows the stated cent-level rule so results are reproducible. Initial catalog/spec/task scope contains no loan or amortization tool; fresh registry/path/draft check remains mandatory before admission.
