# Meeting Cost Calculator — full preparatory specification v0.1

Specification documentation only; no product claim, readiness event or implementation. This document is the normative contract a future production task must satisfy exactly; where it says MUST, a differing behaviour is a defect, not a variation. A live cost meter invites two quiet cheats this specification forbids: accumulating rounded cents tick by tick (the displayed total drifts from the true figure), and measuring elapsed time on the wall clock (a clock change, or a laptop sleep, silently inflates or deflates the meeting). Here the timer stores only exact elapsed time from a monotonic base, and every displayed cost is recomputed from scratch — burn × total elapsed — so the number is reproducible at any instant and immune to tick rounding.

## 1. Goal
Primary audience: team leads and project managers who suspect a recurring meeting costs more than it decides, facilitators running workshops, and anyone preparing a "should this be an email?" argument with a number attached. The recurring problem: a meeting's price is invisible because it is spread across salaries; nobody multiplies eight people's hourly rates by ninety minutes in their head. Success is observable in two moments: before the meeting, the user assembles the roster as up to five groups (headcount × hourly rate, typed or filled from an annual-salary preset), enters a planned duration, and gets the exact planned cost with a per-group breakdown; during the meeting, the user starts the timer and watches the live cost climb from the same roster, pausing for breaks. Non-goals: judging whether a meeting is worthwhile, tracking anyone's actual pay (rates are figures the user enters; presets are labelled illustrative), or integrating with calendars.

Assumptions: a modern desktop or mobile browser; rates are understood to be rough loaded or nominal figures the user chooses; no network at runtime; nothing about the meeting is stored.

## 2. Scope and functional distinction
Included: (a) 1–5 attendee groups, each: headcount (whole, 1–100), hourly rate (0–100,000, at most two decimal places) in the chosen currency, and a preset selector with the closed list of section 4 that fills the rate field; groups can be added and removed (a minimum of one group always remains); (b) a currency selector (NT$, US$, €, £, ¥) that changes only the displayed symbol — no conversion ever; (c) manual mode: duration hours (0–24) and minutes (0–59) with total 1–1,440 minutes; (d) timer mode: start, pause, resume, reset, driven by the monotonic rule of section 4; (e) outputs in both modes: combined burn per hour, cost per minute, total cost, and a per-group breakdown table (headcount, rate, group subtotal, per-person cost for that group), where the manual mode uses the entered duration and the timer mode uses the live elapsed time; (f) the honesty note of section 5 about how mid-meeting roster changes re-price elapsed time. Excluded: named attendees, employee records or any personal data; calendar or video-call integration; reminders and notifications; saving or exporting meetings; multi-currency rosters (one currency per meeting).
Distinction from existing work: Bill Splitter divides a bill that already exists among people paying it; this tool builds a cost from rates × time before any bill exists, and runs live. Work Hours Calculator sums one person's clock-in/clock-out hours for payroll; it has no roster, no rates for a room, and no accumulating cost. The Fuel Cost Calculator's optional split divides a computed trip cost — a secondary display there, unrelated to time-based burn here. No catalog entry, reserved specification or registry task at admission time prices a meeting; the admission check MUST be re-run against the fresh registry before production, and a collision stops the task for coordinator decision rather than a rename.

## 3. User flow and lifecycle
Initial state: manual mode, one group of 5 people at rate 40.00, currency US$, duration 1 hour 0 minutes — the outputs already show a cost of $200.00 and the matching breakdown. Editing any field recomputes every output together. Adding a group appends an empty-rate group (rate blank counts as an error state for that group, per below, until filled or the group is removed).
Timer mode: switching to it shows elapsed 00:00:00, the live cost, and Start. Start begins accumulation; Pause freezes the displayed elapsed and cost exactly (the frozen values do not creep); Resume continues from the frozen elapsed; Reset returns elapsed to zero and stops the clock. Switching between manual and timer modes preserves the roster and each mode's own state for the session.
Roster edits during a running timer are permitted and take effect immediately under the section 5 rule: the live cost is recomputed from the full elapsed time at the new burn, and the visible note reminds the user of exactly that.
Error states: headcount non-whole or outside 1–100; rate negative, above 100,000, more than two decimal places, or blank; duration total 0 minutes or above 1,440 (hours 24 requires minutes 0). While any error stands, outputs freeze at the last valid state marked "out of date"; the timer, if running, keeps accumulating elapsed time (time is not an input error), and its cost display freezes with the same mark until the roster is valid again. Nothing persists across reload; no figure appears in the URL or title.

## 4. Computation rules (normative)
Money and rates are exact decimals (two places); headcounts and minutes are integers; elapsed time in timer mode is stored in whole milliseconds from the monotonic base.
Groups and burn: for groups i with headcount c_i and rate r_i, burn = Σ c_i × r_i (exact). Per-person cost of group i over a duration of T minutes = r_i × T ÷ 60. Group subtotal = c_i × that. Total = burn × T ÷ 60. Cost per minute = burn ÷ 60. Display: all money at 2 dp, round-half-up, applied once per displayed figure from its exact value; the breakdown subtotals are each rounded independently, so displayed subtotals may sum a cent off the displayed total — the table carries the standard rounding note rather than forcing a false reconciliation.
Presets (closed list, annual salary → hourly rate): 40,000 → labelled "≈40k / year"; 70,000; 110,000; 180,000 — the filled rate = annual ÷ 2080 exactly, then rounded half-up to 2 dp at fill time (40,000 → 19.23; 70,000 → 33.65; 110,000 → 52.88; 180,000 → 86.54). 2080 = 52 weeks × 40 hours; both the divisor and the illustrative nature of the figures are stated next to the selector. Choosing "custom" leaves the typed rate untouched.
Manual mode: T = 60 × hours + minutes, 1 ≤ T ≤ 1,440.
Timer mode: let the clock base be performance.now() (monotonic). The timer stores accumulated_ms (sum of completed running intervals) and, while running, interval_start. elapsed_ms = accumulated_ms + (running ? now − interval_start : 0). Cost at any instant = burn × elapsed_ms ÷ 3,600,000 exactly (equivalently burn × elapsed_seconds ÷ 3600), displayed at 2 dp half-up, refreshed at least once per second and on every state change. The displayed cost MUST be recomputed from elapsed_ms on every refresh — incrementing a stored money value by per-tick amounts is forbidden. If the tab is hidden, accumulation continues by the same formula when visible again (monotonic base; no tick counting). The timer has no maximum within a page session; elapsed displays as H:MM:SS with hours unbounded.
Worked fixtures (normative, re-derived by script during drafting; timer fixtures use an injected fake monotonic clock, which the core MUST accept as a substitutable dependency):
- M1: groups (3 @ 60.00) + (2 @ 45.00), manual 0 h 45 min → burn **$270.00/h**, cost per minute **$4.50**, total **$202.50**; per-person: group 1 $45.00, subtotal $135.00; group 2 $33.75, subtotal $67.50.
- M2: preset 70,000 fills rate **33.65**; one group of 4 at that rate, manual 30 minutes → burn **$134.60/h**, total **$67.30**.
- M3: M1's roster on the timer: elapsed 600 s → **$45.00**; elapsed 90 s → **$6.75**; a pause from 90 s to (fake-clock) 10,090 s adds nothing — resuming and advancing 10 s gives elapsed 100 s → **$7.50**.
- M4: one person @ 100.00, manual 8 h 0 min → total **$800.00**.
- M5: one person @ 100.00 on the timer at elapsed 100 s → exact 2.7777… → displayed **$2.78** (single display rounding; a per-second-increment implementation cannot reproduce this reliably and fails the drift test in section 7).

## 5. Implementation and safety
Architecture (future production): a pure core (roster arithmetic, preset fill, duration cost, and the timer state machine over an injected monotonic clock) with zero DOM access; a controller; a thin DOM layer. The preset list, the 2080 divisor, bounds and currency list appear once as auditable data matching section 4. Runtime dependencies: none.
Privacy/security: no network, no storage, no analytics — a meeting's roster and cost are exactly the kind of figures that must not leak into a URL or a log; inputs render as text only; no eval/Function; no external resources.
Honesty rules (normative, testable): presets are labelled illustrative figures the user can replace, never presented as salary data about anyone; the currency selector is a symbol only; the mid-meeting repricing rule is stated where the roster is edited ("cost = current rates × total elapsed time; editing rates re-prices the whole elapsed time — the tool does not track rate history"); the page does not claim the figures are anyone's true compensation cost (loaded costs, taxes and benefits are the user's choice of rate).
Bilingual and accessibility: full zh-TW (default) and English UI, including the breakdown table, the repricing note and the preset labels. Native labelled inputs and buttons; the live cost is text that updates without stealing focus (updates announced politely at most on pause/stop and on demand, not every second — a live region that speaks every second is an accessibility defect); keyboard-complete flow.
Performance bounds: five groups of arithmetic per refresh; a 1 Hz display tick. Toolchain: Node 24, zero npm dependencies, house layout (public/, public/en/, core modules, node:test suites, build/check/serve scripts); runs offline from file:// or any static host.

## 6. Independent nine-aspect product rubric
Each aspect is scored 0–100 by a reviewer who is not the author, against the same fixed candidate commit; every aspect MUST reach ≥90 with all mandatory checks complete, otherwise the verdict is revision. Unrun checks stay NOT_RUN and block acceptance.
- functionality: both languages and both modes; fixtures M1–M5 reproduced exactly, including the breakdown figures, the preset fill and the pause behaviour.
- boundaries: groups 1 and 5 work, a 6th is refused; headcount 1 and 100 accepted, 0, 101 and 2.5 refused; rate 0 accepted, negative and 100,000.01 refused; duration 1 and 1,440 minutes accepted, 0 and 1,441 refused (24 h with minutes > 0 refused); timer elapsed crossing one hour displays and prices correctly.
- privacy: zero network after load; no storage; roster and figures absent from URL/title and never transmitted.
- security: field content containing markup renders inert; no external resources; no eval/Function.
- usability: a team lead assembles a six-person two-group roster and gets a planned 45-minute cost without instructions, and can read from the breakdown which group costs most per person (spot-check).
- accessibility: labelled native controls; breakdown is a real table with headers; the live region does not announce every second; keyboard-complete; frozen/paused states are text, not colour alone.
- localization-seo: zh-TW and en pages complete and equivalent (including preset labels, repricing note and rounding note); correct lang attributes, titles, descriptions and same-language home links.
- toolchain-provenance: zero-dependency build reproduces byte-identical output on Node 24; presets, the 2080 divisor and bounds appear once as auditable data matching section 4; the timer demonstrably uses a monotonic base (a faked wall-clock jump in tests changes nothing).
- compatibility: current Chrome/Edge/Firefox/Safari desktop and mobile-width layout; scripted-clock fixtures give identical costs across browsers.

## 7. Required tests and timing
Core unit tests with independently derived expectations: (a) fixtures M1–M5 exactly, every output figure; (b) preset fills: all four annual figures map to the section 4 rates, and editing a filled rate afterwards behaves as a plain rate; (c) the timer state machine on the fake clock: start/pause/resume/reset sequences, pause spanning a large fake interval (M3), elapsed accumulation across several intervals summing exactly, reset while running; (d) the drift property: cost displayed after a scripted irregular tick sequence (refreshes at uneven fake times) always equals the exact formula from total elapsed — an implementation accumulating per-tick rounded cents produces a different figure on the M5-style sequence and is caught; (e) bounds: every section 4 limit at and beyond; breakdown rounding: a constructed roster whose independently rounded subtotals differ from the rounded total by one cent, with the note present.
GUI/evidence tests (desktop, real browser): a real timer run of ~65 seconds compared against the formula within one display cent; manual and timer modes switched back and forth with state preserved; a roster edit mid-run repricing per the stated rule; stale-state behaviour on an invalid rate while the timer runs. Timing: full core suite under 60 seconds; no network in any test.
Explicitly NOT_RUN at specification stage: all product tests above; this document claims document completeness only.
